## Mechanic

Roast all the cauliflower hard, then hold back a cup of the best florets for the top. The roasted edges give the soup its depth and the spices toast on the pan instead of tasting raw in the pot. Salt pork renders the fat the onion cooks in, and its crisp bits go on top. Ginger goes in after the onion softens and cooks only a minute, so it stays bright rather than turning bitter; weigh it, **15–30 g**, because grated ginger packs unevenly in a spoon.

## Ingredients

- 680 g (1½ lb) cauliflower, cut into 2.5 cm (1 inch) florets, about 1.5 L (6 heaping cups)
- 3 cloves garlic
- 200 g salt pork, diced
- 30 ml (2 tbsp) olive oil
- 1 tsp ground turmeric
- 1 tsp ground cumin
- ⅛ tsp crushed red pepper flakes, optional
- ½ tsp kosher salt, plus more to taste
- 1 medium onion, chopped
- 15–30 g (1–2 tbsp) fresh ginger, peeled and finely grated
- 750 ml (3 cups) vegetable stock
- 60 ml (¼ cup) full-fat canned coconut milk, shaken well, plus more for drizzling
- 2 tbsp chopped cilantro

## Method

**Soak the salt pork.** Cover the diced salt pork with cold water and soak for 30–60 minutes to draw out some of the salt. Drain and pat dry.

**Spice the cauliflower.** Heat the oven to 230°C (450°F). Smash the garlic cloves with the side of a knife. Put the cauliflower and garlic in a large bowl and drizzle with 30 ml (2 tbsp) olive oil. Toss until the florets are evenly coated. Mix the turmeric, cumin, salt and pepper flakes in a small bowl, sprinkle over the cauliflower, and toss again until every floret is yellow.

**Roast.** Spread the cauliflower on a large rimmed baking sheet in a single layer. Roast in the centre of the oven until browned at the edges and tender to a knife, 25–30 minutes, turning the florets once or twice. Set aside 250 ml (1 cup) of the best-looking florets for the top.

**Render the salt pork.** Meanwhile, put the salt pork in a cold medium pot and set it over medium heat. Cook, stirring now and then, until the fat has rendered and the pieces are golden and crisp at the edges, 8–10 minutes. Lift them out with a slotted spoon and set aside, leaving about 15 ml (1 tbsp) of fat in the pot.

**Build the pot.** Add the onion to the fat and cook until translucent, 2–3 minutes. Add the ginger and stir until fragrant, about 1 minute; don't let it brown.

**Simmer.** Add the stock and the rest of the roasted cauliflower and garlic, scraping in any spices left on the pan. Bring to a boil, cover, and simmer on low for 15 minutes.

**Blend.** Blend with an immersion blender until smooth, or in a countertop blender in small batches with the lid vented. Stir in the coconut milk. Taste before adding salt; the salt pork brings plenty.

## To serve

Topped with the reserved florets, the crisp salt pork, the cilantro, and a drizzle of coconut milk.

## Notes

- Vegetarian: skip the salt pork, cook the onion in 5 ml (1 tsp) olive oil, and expect to add more salt at the end.
- Too thick after blending: loosen with stock, a splash at a time.
- Keeps refrigerated 4 days. Freezes well before the coconut milk goes in; stir it in after reheating.
