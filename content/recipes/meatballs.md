A meatball is a satisfying, compact vessel for flavour and nutrition. They are a great way to use less meat or no meat in a meal. In basic terms, a meatball is a protein base and binders, moisteners (panade), and flavourings. You can take a cheap cut of meat or normally dry plant protein and dress it up into a savoury and healthy food.

You can vary this recipe quite a lot, and have it still work well. For example, I often skip the parmesan or chicken stock, depending what I have in the house at the time.

### Method:  

1. Make a panade with the liquids and bread crumbs, adding in the. Sour cream can be added as part of the liquid (or buttermilk, or cream, or milk). Use grated onions and crushed garlic. Should be just moistened, so add liquid slowly.
2. Grind spices. Add spices and herbs to the panade.
3. Mix thoroughly (paddle, or by hand)
4. Brown in the oven (190-220C) and cool (or use the broiler on low).

## Asian style

- Sub hoisin (3%) / soy (6.5%) / ginger (1%), sesame oil (1%)for some of the liquid
- Adjust salt (soy replaces). Skip parm (optionally).
- Balls should be slightly thicker than Italian style (so scale back liquid)
- Glaze: 60ml hoisin, 30ml ketchup, 15ml honey/syrup, 30ml vinegar/rice, 5ml sesame oil, 15ml soy sauce.

Cover with sesame seeds and scallions
