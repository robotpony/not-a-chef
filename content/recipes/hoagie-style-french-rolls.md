A hoagie roll is a sort of baguette used in sub style sandwiches. This roll could swap in for beef dips, subs, poboys, or even hamburger buns (halved). They have some body and structure, are a tiny bit chewy, and have some crust. They hold up well to robust sandwiches, and crunch when you take a bite.

#### Main ingredients:

- 500g (2 cups) 2% milk
- 15g (2 tsp) instant yeast
- 15g (2 tsp) diastatic malt powder
- 800g bread flour (or AP, or a 50:50 mix of whole/refined flour)
- 20g (about 1 tbsp) sugar
- 40g olive oil or melted (and partly cooled) butter
- 15g (2 tsp) salt

#### Egg wash:

- 1 egg white
- Splash of water (1-2 tsp)

#### Other:

- Cornmeal sprinkle
- Sesame seeds
- Herbs (super optional)

### Directions:

1. Heat milk to about 40C (but not much more) on the stove or in the microwave. You can optionally cut the milk 50:50 with water if you want a lighter dough, or use heavy cream if you want a denser, softer crumb.
2. In a large bowl (or stand mixer bowl) add the yeast and malt powder to milk and let sit for 5-10 minutes. It should end up frothy. If you don’t see any significant bubbles by 20 minutes, the yeast isn’t viable and you should start again with fresh yeast.[\[a\]](#cmnt1)
3. Add the rest of the ingredients to the bowl (less ½ cup of the flour or so) and turn on the mixer using the dough hook, or start mixing by hand. This dough is a bit sticky, but should be smooth before proofing.

    1. If using a mixer, mix until combined and then for about 10 minutes until the dough looks smooth and is no longer sticking (as much) to the bowl. Add the remaining flour (and up to ½ cup more)  if the dough continues to be too sticky.
    2. If mixing by hand, mix until combined with a spatula, adding more flour if needed. Pour out onto a floured surface and knead for about 10 minutes or until the dough is smooth and passes the window test. I don’t often use the slap method, instead I opt for the fold-over method (fold over itself, rotate 45 degrees, repeat).

4. Bulk ferment, covered, in a warm part of the kitchen for about 1 hour, or until doubled. Alternatively, you can ferment in the fridge for 12-24 hours.
5. Split into 8 equal portions, 150-200g per portion. Flatten and spread into a rectangle slightly longer than your desired length. I usually aim for rectangles that are about 25cm by 15cm and 2cm thick. Roll the rectangles into a tight log and seal up the seam.
6. Place the dough logs onto a sheet pan with parchment and cornmeal. Cover with plastic, another pan, or a towel and let rise until doubled (about an hour) until they pass the poke test.
7. Preheat your oven to 190C/375F and make the egg wash. I use the convection setting on my oven, as it promotes more even browning.
8. Score the rolls along their length (hogies are scored off to one side, the entire length of the roll, but feel free to get creative). The scored portion helps control how the roll expands while cooking, rather than it figuring out how to expand on its own.
9. Brush the rolls with your egg wash and add any extra ingredients you like (nuts, sesame seeds, oatmeal, herbs).
10. On a pan below your middle rack, add a pan and a handful of ice cubes to produce some steam. If you happen to have a food-safe spray bottle, spray the rolls before putting them in, and half way through.
11. Bake for 20 minutes on the middle rack, rotating the pan about half way through. You’re aiming for a golden brown exterior and interior temperature of 90-95C (or about 200F).
12. Cool on a wire rack until completely cooled. If you’re aiming for that chewy hoagie texture, throw the rolls in the fridge in a paper or plastic bag for 24 hours once cooled.
