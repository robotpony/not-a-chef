This is a quick and effective way to marinade chicken and pork. The mayo ensure spices don't burn, and it adheres to the cut surprisingly well.

## Mechanic

Mayonaiuse coats the meat evenly and holds the seasonings in contact throughout marinating, producing more consistent browning and a tender surface. The baking soda effect is mild; the big win is surface adhesion. The result is that meats and spices are less prone to burning.

## Ingredients

- 50g mayonnaise (Helmans or Kewpie)
- 2 cloves garlic (10g minced)
- ~1g black pepper (10 cranks)
- 1 teaspoons dried oregano
- zest of 1 lemon (or a squeeze of lemon juice)
- 3g kosher salt
- 1g MSG

## Method

Remove fat, skin, and butterfly larger chicken breasts or pork chops. Pound out slightly, to a desirable shape.

Combine all ingredients and mix well. Coat chicken and marinate for 30 minutes minimum, up to 8 hours in the fridge.

Grill, broil, or pan-fry as usual. The mayo coating browns and crisps on contact with heat. Cook to 63C/145F and let rest (it should peak at 66C / 150F).

## To serve

Serve chicken or pork with, rice, a salad, or on a bun.

## Notes

- I was skeptical about this technique until Keji explained that mayo is a fat that is emulsified with acid, salt, and spice
- Tested as a pure marinade (not salted separately first). Worked well; slightly salty, very tasty.
- Reduce salt  if using a thinner cut that will marinate longer.
- Kewpie is an thick, savoury, and enriched Japanese mayonnaise (packaged in a classic squeeze bottle)
- Untested, but Kenji mentioned it works really well with teriyaki, so this should work well for hearty fish like salmon
