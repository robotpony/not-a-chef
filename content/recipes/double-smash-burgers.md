## Mechanic

A cold ball of beef is **smashed once, hard, as soon as it hits a very hot pan**, so the whole underside browns at the same time. The crust is the point: a thin patty has more browned surface per gram than a thick one, and two of them stacked make up the bulk. Smash early, while the beef is raw and cold; pressing a patty that's already cooking squeezes out the juice.

## Burgers

- ½ tsp vegetable oil
- 455 g (1 lb) ground beef, 80/20, cold, in four 115 g (4 oz) loose balls
- Kosher salt and black pepper
- 2 slices American cheese
- 2 soft burger buns, toasted
- ¼ small white onion, diced fine
- Dill pickle slices
- Ketchup and mustard, or [[Bruce's burger sauce]]

Toast the cut sides of the buns in a dry non-stick pan over medium heat until golden, and set them aside. Put the diced onion on the bottom buns.

Heat a cast iron pan or griddle over high until it just starts to smoke. Wipe it with the oil.

Put two balls of beef in the pan, well apart. Smash each one flat right away with a stiff metal spatula, leaning on it with your other hand, until it's about 6 mm (¼ inch) thick and wider than the bun. Season the tops with salt and pepper.

Cook without moving until the edges are deep brown and lacy and the top has turned mostly grey, about 2 minutes. Scrape under each patty with the spatula edge to keep the crust, and flip. Lay a slice of cheese on one patty, stack the other on top, and cook 30 seconds more.

Set the stack on the onion on a bottom bun. Add pickles and sauce, and close it. Repeat with the other two balls.

## To serve

Straight from the pan.

## Variations

### Singles
One 115 g patty per bun, cheese on top. Less crust per bite; feeds four.

### Triple
Three 75 g patties, cheese between each. Even more crust, and thinner patties cook in about 90 seconds.

## Notes

- Smash on parchment, or with a second spatula stacked on the first, if the beef sticks to the spatula.
- Don't crowd the pan. Two patties at a time keeps it hot enough to brown.
- A stiff, sharp-edged spatula matters more than the press. A flimsy one leaves the crust on the pan.
