Kālua pig and cabbage is Hawaiʻi's ultimate comfort food: slow-cooked pork shoulder until it's fall-apart tender, then finished with wilted cabbage and a splash of soy sauce. Traditionally cooked in an imu (underground oven); this slow cooker version is a weeknight-friendly stand-in.

## Ingredients

- 1.5 to 2.3 kg pork butt
- Flaky sea salt
- Freshly ground black pepper
- 1 tablespoon Worcestershire sauce
- 800 g green cabbage (about 3/4 head), chopped into 2.5 cm pieces
- 60 ml reduced-sodium soy sauce
- Cooked white rice, for serving

## Method

1. Generously season pork with salt and pepper on all sides, rubbing it into the meat.
2. Place pork in a slow cooker. Add Worcestershire sauce and toss to coat. Cover and cook on low until tender enough to shred with a fork, 7–9 hours depending on size.
3. Shred meat with two forks, leaving the drippings in the slow cooker. Add one-third of the cabbage. Once it cooks down, stir and add another third; repeat until all the cabbage is wilted, about 30 minutes total.
4. Continue cooking on low until the pork and cabbage are very tender, 30–60 minutes more. Add soy sauce and stir to combine; taste and adjust salt and pepper.
5. Ladle pork, cabbage, and some juices over rice to serve.

## Notes

- Liquid smoke (a few drops rubbed into the pork with the salt) gets you closer to the traditional imu smoke flavour.
- The drippings are key. Don't drain them before adding the cabbage.
