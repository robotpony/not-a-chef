This is more of a method than a specific recipe, and the dough can be used for several similar types of breads (including focaccia, buns, and loaves) by varying the rise and oven time. The baguette method uses a narrow shape and steam to form a fantastic crust. This recipe and method is based on [Ethan Chlebowski’s excellent tutorial video](https://www.ethanchlebowski.com/cooking-techniques-recipes/sandwich-baguettes). Also see [Paul Holywood’s recipe](https://www.paulhollywood.com/post/baguettes).

#### Ingredients:

- 10 grams of instant yeast
- 375 grams of water (~30C)
- 490 grams all-purpose or bread flour
- 10 grams of diastatic malt powder  
    Optional for browning and texture.
- 10 grams of non iodized salt

### Method:

This is a high hydration bread, which means it’s super sticky and easy to deflate (so be gentle). It can be easier to knead this dough in a mixer with a dough hook.

5-6 hours

1.

Optionally, bloom the yeast in the water and 50 grams of flour.

10 minutes

2.

Add the flour, salt, and optional diastatic malt powder to a large mixing bowl and combine with a fork or whisk. If you don’t have malt powder, replace it with 10 grams of flour to maintain the hydration level.

5 minutes

3.

Add water and yeast mixture and stir with a spatula to combine, scraping down the bowl and folding the dough into itself as you work.

5-10 minutes

4.

Use the [stretch and fold method](https://www.theclevercarrot.com/2020/05/how-to-stretch-and-fold-sourdough/), repeating it 4-5 times every 30 minutes until the dough stretches thin enough to see through.

Apply the technique gently, pulling each corner of the dough and folding it over itself. Using a wetted bowl scraper makes this a bit faster than by hand (but either method will work).

2 - 2 ½ hours

Repeating at 30 minute intervals. 

5.

Divide dough into 6 portions (150 grams each) on a floured work surface. Form portions into rough rectangles, working further with each rectangle and stretching to about 15cm (6 inches) wide. Fold the rectangles in rough thirds into the middle, forming a sealed roll of dough, sealing the seams gently with your fingers.

Roll out the rolls, tapering the ends to the traditional points (or not, if you want more sandwich-friendly loaves).

Place rolls on parchment paper and cover with a slightly dampened towel.

15 minutes

6.

Proof for about an hour, or until about almost doubled in size.

1 hour

7.

Before proofing completes, preheat the oven to 230C (450F) with convection on (if you have it).  

Add a hotel pan (or casserole dish) of water below the middle rack.

Score the rolls with a sharp knife or razor 45°along the full length at least 1cm (~½ an inch) deep. Spray or sprinkle water over the rolls to encourage browning.

30 minutes (before baking)

8.

Bake for roughly 20 minutes, rotating before the crust is fully formed, somewhere before the 10 minute mark. You can tell the loaves are done when they’re a dark golden brown and sound hollow when tapped.

Let cool fully on a wire rack before slicing.

20 minutes  
(per tray)
