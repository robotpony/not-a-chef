## Mechanic

**Scotch bonnets are non-negotiable** for authentic heat and fruitiness; habaneros are the closest substitute. Blending everything wet (citrus, vinegar, soy) produces a paste that coats and penetrates well. Allspice and cinnamon are the warmth backbone — don't reduce them.

## Ingredients

- 4–6 scallions (green onions), roughly chopped
- 4–6 scotch bonnet chillies, stems removed (seeds in for hot, seeds out for medium)
- 1 tbsp fresh thyme leaves (or 1 tsp dried)
- 1 tbsp ground allspice
- 1 tsp ground cinnamon
- 1 tbsp fresh ginger, grated
- 4 cloves garlic
- 1 small onion, roughly chopped
- 1 tbsp honey
- 2 tbsp rice vinegar
- 2 tbsp dark soy sauce
- juice of 1 lime
- juice of 1 lemon
- 2 tbsp orange juice
- salt

## Method

Blend all ingredients together until a rough paste forms. Adjust consistency with a splash more orange juice if too thick.

Taste — it should be hot, fragrant, sweet, and sour in roughly equal balance. Add more soy for salt, honey for sweetness, or citrus for brightness.

Coat meat thoroughly. Marinate in the fridge for at least 4 hours, or overnight.

Grill or roast at high heat for charred edges and a sticky, caramelised exterior.

## To serve

With rice and peas, coleslaw, and fried plantain.
