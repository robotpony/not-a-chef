## Mechanic

The **1:1:1 braise ratio** (water : soy : mirin) keeps the pork moist and seasoned through. Refrigerating in the liquid overnight firms the meat and deepens the flavour; slicing cold gives cleaner cuts. Pork belly braises to 85°C for a yielding, fatty result; loin braises to 60°C for a leaner, cleaner slice.

## Ingredients

- 500g pork belly or pork loin (towards the rib end)
- 250ml water
- 250ml soy sauce
- 250ml mirin
- 2 tablespoons sugar
- 2 teaspoons neutral oil
- 2 large garlic cloves, crushed
- 3 green onions, cut into 5 cm lengths
- 5 cm piece fresh ginger, thinly sliced

## Method

Sear pork on all sides in oil over medium-high heat until browned, about 3 minutes per side. Remove and deglaze the pan with a splash of water.

Return pork. Add soy, mirin, water, sugar, garlic, green onion, and ginger. Bring to a low simmer.

Cover and braise over low heat for 1–2 hours. Target 85°C internal for belly, 60°C for loin.

Cool slightly, then transfer pork and braising liquid to a container. Refrigerate at least 24 hours.

Slice as needed. Reheat in a dry pan, in a little oil, in stock, or in the oven.

## To serve

Over ramen, in rice bowls, alongside pickled vegetables. The braising liquid doubles as a tare for ramen broth.

## Notes

- Freeze leftovers after 3 days.
- Belly is richer and more forgiving on temperature; loin dries out if overcooked.
