This is one of my favourite stews. It's slightly sweet and savoury, and completely delicious.

## Mechanic

The **paprika load here is 4 tablespoons per kg of meat**, not the 1 tablespoon most North American recipes use. That's what makes this taste like goulash and not like beef stew with a little paprika. Caraway is more traditional than cumin; either works but they produce different results. Bacon fat is optional; fatty pork shoulder brings enough of its own.

## Ingredients

- 3 tablespoons fat (lard, fat cap, olive oil, or bacon fat)
- 1 kg cubed stew meat (pork shoulder or beef chuck), lightly floured
- 2 yellow onions, medium diced
- 3 cloves garlic, finely minced
- 1 red bell pepper, seeded and medium diced
- 3 vine-ripe tomatoes, cored and medium diced (or 1 × 400g tin)
- 1½ teaspoons caraway seeds (or ground cumin)
- 4 tablespoons sweet Hungarian paprika
- 250ml red wine (1 cup)
- 1 litre beef stock (4 cups)
- 2 bay leaves
- 2 russet potatoes, peeled and cut into 2.5 cm chunks
- 3 carrots, peeled and large diced
- salt and pepper

Optional: 1 cup each large diced celeriac and turnip.

## Method

Heat fat in a wide, heavy pot over medium-high. Brown the floured meat in batches without crowding, about 4 minutes per side. Set aside.

Reduce heat to medium. Add onion and cook until soft and translucent, about 8 minutes. Add garlic and bell pepper; cook 2 more minutes.

Stir in paprika and caraway. Add tomatoes and cook until they break down slightly, about 3 minutes.

Add wine and scrape up any browned bits. Add stock, bay leaves, and browned meat. Bring to a simmer.

Cover and cook on low for 1 hour. Add potatoes, carrots, and optional root vegetables. Continue cooking until vegetables are tender and the stew is thick, 30–45 more minutes.

Season with salt and pepper. Remove bay leaves.

## To serve

With egg noodles, sour cream, and crusty bread. The stew deepens overnight.

## Variations

### Prep-day goulash with mushrooms and sherry
A version worked out from first principles on a prep day, which turned out better than expected. Cube a pork shoulder (2 cm), season, toss in oil, and broil until one side browns; keep the drippings. In a large pot, cook 6–10 sliced cremini mushrooms in a little oil with 100 ml water until the water evaporates and they brown. Add 4 roughly diced onions (any mix of white, red, shallot, and leek) and a sliced carrot and cook down, then a head of garlic, roughly diced. Add 30 ml tomato paste and cook until it sticks, then 2 heaping tablespoons sweet paprika and 1 tsp crushed fennel or caraway. Deglaze with 125 ml sherry, then add 500 ml chicken or beef stock, 15 ml Worcestershire, a large tin of tomatoes, and the pork with its drippings. Hold at a bare simmer, stirring regularly, until the pork is fork-tender, 2–5 hours. Finish with browning sauce as needed, salt, MSG, and up to 50 ml cider vinegar to balance.

## Notes

- Pork shoulder is more forgiving than chuck; equally good result.
