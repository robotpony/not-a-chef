Makes ~3 medium pizzas.

This is an improved dough tuned to work easily with a 6L stand mixer. It’s high-hydration and can be sticky, but the extra water provides a better ferment and lift. It also works well for focaccia with the addition of more oil in the pan and whatever toppings you enjoy.

Note: I don’t suggest 00 flour for this recipe unless you have a pizza steel or oven that can reach 300C+. AP seems to work better in a home oven, though kneading and cooking times are a bit longer.

## Ingredients:

Combine and let sit until foamy:

- 400 grams water (warmish)
- 20 grams olive oil
- 10 grams diastolic malt powder
- 6 grams yeast

Add to stand mixer bowl:

- 600 grams AP flour
- 12 grams salt

If you can’t find malt powder, replace it with regular flour. The diastolic malt helps develop flavour more quickly and can be approximated with a bit more ferment time and can be found in specialty baking stores and online.

## Method:

1. Combine yeast, malt powder, water, and olive oil in a pourable container and let sit for 10-20 minutes. A light foam should appear if the yeast is still viable.
2. With the flour and salt in your mixer bowl, add the yeast slurry and set the mixer to low (1 on our KitchenAid). Mix until combined.
3. Once combined, set the mixer to medium and knead until a smooth dough forms (5-10 minutes). You may need to scrape down the dough hook, but generally it will work itself out with some speed adjustments. Note that your mixer may bounce its way off your counter, so don’t leave it unattended.
4. Using a dough scraper, pull the dough out into a container 3-4x larger than the dough. Add olive oil to the container and form a rough ball.
5. Let the dough prove until doubled (or so), which takes about an hour.
6. If you have time to ferment the dough it improves the texture and flavour. This can take 24h-72h, depending on how much time you have. Make sure the dough is well oiled (or covered in plastic wrap) and place it in the back of your refrigerator for up to 3 days.
7. If you’ve fermented your dough, remove from the fridge and let it come back up to room temperature.
8. Using the lift and fold method, stretch and fold the dough in the bowl over itself 4 times. Let rest for 30 minutes and repeat. This aligns and strengthens the dough (making for larger bubbles). This can be repeated up to 4 times, but I generally only do it a few times.
9. Divide into 3 portions and place on oiled and corn-meal (or semolina) lined pizza or sheet pans. If the dough isn’t stretching easily, let it sit for 10-20 minutes and try again.

There are a few ways to bake pizza dough, depending on your toppings and oven. Generally, it’s easier to hit the grandma-style pizza in a home oven (176C/350F for 30-40 minutes), but you can also get a thinner pizza to rise nicely with some practice at higher temperatures (220C/430F or above). The hotter method would produce a bit more rise and crunch, but both work quite well.
