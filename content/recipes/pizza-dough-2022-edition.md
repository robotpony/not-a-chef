This is another iteration of my pizza dough recipe, based on some experimentation in late 2021. This is a medium-high hydration dough, optionally reinforced with additional gluten for structure and chew. I skip the autolyse step now, which I’ve replaced with a longer cold rise time. This recipe is based on [Flour Water Salt Yeast](https://www.amazon.ca/Flour-Water-Salt-Yeast-Fundamentals/dp/160774273X), with gluten ratios lifted from recipes at Serious Eats (aiming at ~13-14% gluten).

## Mechanic

This dough runs at **~75% hydration**, with vital wheat gluten added to push the flour blend toward ~13-14% protein, closer to bread flour, for more structure and chew. Skipping the autolyse in favour of a long cold ferment (24 hours minimum, up to 5-6 days) develops flavour instead, while keeping the mixing itself to one pass.

## Ingredients

- 1000g AP flour
- 20g vital wheat gluten (replace with flour if needed)
- 25g kosher salt
- 20g granulated sugar
- 7g instant yeast (~1 tsp)
- 750g warm water (30-35C)
- 30g olive oil
- 10-20g olive oil, for the proofing bucket

## Method

1. In a stand mixer bowl, add the flour, gluten, salt, sugar, and yeast. Stir to combine on the mixer’s lowest setting, using the dough hook.
2. Drizzle in the water until combined. Add the olive oil until combined.
3. Once combined, knead the dough on medium until it forms a smooth dough that is free of the bowl. You may need to scrape down the hook and bowl a few times with your dough scraper, or raise and lower the bowl. This usually takes 5-10 minutes, and you’ll know the dough is ready when it forms a cohesive and smooth ball (or lump).
4. Oil a proofing bucket and transfer the dough to it. Cover and refrigerate for a minimum of 24 hours, 48-72 hours is ideal (and up to 5-6 days).

## Making pizzas

1. Remove 300g (small) - 450g (medium) chunks of dough from the proofing bucket, and form balls on a lightly floured counter. Let these balls come up to room temperature (and rise a bit). This takes about 2 hours (depending how warm your kitchen is), but use the finger test to determine when they’re ready. Dough that isn’t proofed enough will not stretch well, and will bubble up more during the bake.
2. Roll out or otherwise flatten each dough ball on a floured counter. Add to a lightly greased pan or ungreased parchment, add toppings, and bake in a hot oven (233C/450F) on the middle rack. If you have a pizza steel/stone or cast/carbon steel pan, you’ll get a slightly better crust in less time.
3. Bake for 10-15 minutes, or until the crust is golden and crispy (checking the bottom).

## Notes

### Making other breads

When you have leftover pizza dough you can make other breads with it. And as usually your leftovers are more than 72 hours cold fermented, you’ll get something closer to a sourdough.

Focaccia: bring dough to room temperature, longer rise, lots of oil.

Ciabatta: (Italian baguettes) form short loaves with the letter fold technique. Longer rise. Cooking in a vessel can result in a better crust.

Baguette: similar to Ciabatta, but also vented and sprayed with additional water.

Loaf: With loose dough pull corners over itself 5-10 times, rotating between corners. Invert and form a tight dough ball much like a bun or pizza, let rise. Drop in a parchment-lined Dutch oven and score.

### Internal temperature guide:

- 88C (190F): slightly under
- 94C (200F): fully done

### Troubleshooting

- If the dough isn’t crispy or cooked on the bottom, add it to a medium-hot teflon pan and finish the crust.
- For future pizzas, try different pans, steels, stones, and positions in the oven. Try to use the hottest temperature possible, to get the best structure and chew.
- Electric ovens recover poorly from being opened, so making more than 2-3 will often require a bunch of recharge time to get that temp back up again.
