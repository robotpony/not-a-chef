This is Bruce’s simple, classic beef stew. I prefer to make stew without the extra vegetables, which instead get added when serving. We often add frozen peas, or have it over roasted potatoes and veg. Occasionally I’ll even pair stew with KD, for a nostalgic 80s extend-a-meal effect.

#### Ingredients:

- 1 (1-2 kg / 2-3 lbs) chuck roast, trimmed and cubed
- 2 large yellow onions, diced
- 1 small bag of crimini mushrooms
- 2 leeks, diced
- 2-3 cloves of garlic, minced
- 2 tbsp tomato paste
- 4 cups chicken or beef stock
- 1-2 cups of bright and drinkable red wine
- 1 tbsp Worcestershire sauce
- 1-2 tbsp herbs de provence in a cheese cloth (or bouquet garni)
- Salt/pepper to taste
- ½ cup AP Flour or other starch
- 1-2 tbsp shortening, bacon fat, or natural oil.

#### Optional/bonus ingredients:

- 1 tsp smoked paprika
- 1 tsp accent
- 1-2 tbsp soy sauce
- 2-3 tsp fish sauce

### Directions:

1. Cube and trim the chuck roast, and toss in a large bowl. Season liberally with salt, pepper, and (optionally) smoked paprika and. Toss with a few tablespoons of flour (if using) until coated.
2. In a large Dutch oven or pressure cooker, add fat and seer the floured beef cubes on medium/medium high, being careful not to burn the sucs (which form the fond once deglazed). Remove and reserve the beef cubes in a dish that can catch the drippings.
3. Add the diced mushrooms with some fat and cook until they express their water, then until they’re browning.
4. Add the leeks and onions with any remaining fat, and cook until translucent. Add tomato paste and combine, stirring until fragrant. Add garlic and cook until softened. Deglaze the pot with the wine, scraping until the brown bits are all removed. Add the Worcestershire, and stock, beef and drippings to the pot and bring to a simmer.
5. In a cheese cloth (or stainless steel tea infuser), add the herbs de provence or your favourite stew spice blend. If you have fresh herbs, a bouquet garni can replace any dried herbs de provence.
6. Cook until the meat is tender.

    1. If using a dutch oven: simmer as low as you can on the back of the stove for 2-3 hours, until the beef is tender.
    2. If using a pressure cooker, pressurise on the highest setting (2 on the Fissler) and cook for 30 minutes. Depressurize naturally if possible.

7. Season to taste once tender, and thicken with remaining flour or starch as needed. I prefer to reduce the stew rather than thicken, but flour/starch will shortcut this depending on how much time you have to let it sit on the stove.

Making this as a minimal stew (without the extra veg) lets you simmer it for longer without ruining the more tender veg. This allows for a much fresher tasting stew, with more serving options.
