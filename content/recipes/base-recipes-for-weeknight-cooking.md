Base recipes for weeknight cooking

It’s common for people to prepare entire meals for the week or month, or to make a large meal on the weekend and use it for leftovers. Preparing foods in bulk can save money and time, but the problem with preparing a single meal for an entire week is that you have to eat a single meal for an entire week. This works for some people, but not for me.

Less common to the home kitchen is to do what restaurants do, which is to prepare sauces and bases, as well as other washed and cut ingredients. These simpler preparations can be adapted into several recipes quickly. The bases are easy to scale up, and they often hold well in the refrigerator or freezer, though restaurants rarely freeze their prep to maintain texture and flavour. At home, however, there are many sauces and bases that freeze exceptionally well.

If you look back at the art of preserves you’ll see a lot of foods that serve as bases for many different meals. Things like red sauce, beans, gravies, boiled meats, pickles, vegetables, and so on. The practice is less common in kitchens today, and the art of canning both requires investment and practice, as well as specialised recipes that preserve well.

I’ve found that by following similar principles, but using the fridge and freezer, that I can have ingredients for dozens of meals at the ready. I like to call this proactive laziness, but honestly it’s proactive gluttony as well, as I love to eat a variety of fantastic foods with as little effort as possible.

## Some examples of adapting a base into quick meals

### Precooked ground protein bases

The [cooked beef/mushroom mix](https://docs.google.com/document/d/18B_6jJlS8MKhUsXeGs2gdse1qjwkUURhE54Xlw64Nuk/edit#heading=h.3fsk0afek31n) base is a great example and one of my favourites, as it can be transformed into so many different meals. The mix itself includes some form of the holy trinity, garlic, and mushrooms, and can be pushed towards pretty much any cuisine. We use this mix to make several quick meals:

- American style meat sauce, adding the beef to a quick marinara, or an already prepared frozen red sauce
- It makes a great base for a ravioli or tortellini filling (adding ricotta and herbs)
- Taco beef, by adding half a tin of minced chipotles in adobo, and some cumin, which can be adapted to

    - taco salads and tacos
    - burritos, quesadillas
    - nachos
    - taco soup
    - taco pizza
    - (etc.)

- A quick Canadian chilli, adding the taco beef to a few cans of beans, some red sauce (or whole tinned tomatoes), great with

    - mac & cheese
    - over fries
    - over salad

- Szechuan beef, by adding Szechuan peppers, 5 spice, soy, and some ginger, great over

    - rice bowls
    - asian style noodles
    - eggs

- Bulgogi style beef, by adding a tin of asian pears, ginger, soy, and 5 spice, also great for applications like Szechuan beef
- Cottage pie, by making gravy out of the beef, adding frozen veg, and topping with mash

I freeze my mince bases in 500ml and 250ml deli containers, which are portion sizes for 2-4 people, or 1-2, depending on the adaptation. As I normally prepare it using a pressure cooker, it’s extra tender and deeply seasoned. You can apply the reverse sear method to it if you want some browning, by heating it on high and stirring regularly. It will express any remaining water, reduce, and start browning in about 5 minutes.

### Patties, sausages, and meatballs

I try to keep a dozen or so ground [chicken patties](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1_FNFVNbjRDOEdbD1oU5-5e-PlBo4euAkmzS07YfX2bI/edit#heading=h.3fsk0afek31n) in the freezer, for quick burgers, sandwiches, and noodle or rice bowls. The patties are made from ground chicken, with some added water and elevated pH, making the patties a bit more springy, and more substantial feeling than straight ground chicken (similar to Vietnamese or chinese beef balls).

I also like to keep a kilo or so of breakfast sausage in the freezer, either in patty or hand-made link form. These are great for breakfast sandwiches and wraps, but are also great in a quick mac & cheese, chilli, or stir fry. They tend towards a mildly sweet and fennel-y flavour.

Meatballs are a fantastic freezer food, if frozen carefully. They can dress up a quick red sauce or pizza, they make great sandwiches, and they work surprisingly well in Italian or French soups.

### Stocks and base gravies

Base gravies are a super power that I learned from a few BIR (British Indian Restaurant) streamers, who taught the art of take out style Indian dishes. You can make base gravies for other cuisines, similar to how Thai cooks use curry pastes, or Japanese cooks use curry blocks. These little flavour bombs.

A base gravy can be adapted into almost any takeout style Indian dish. Each adaptation is approximately half base gravy, added to a more specific spice blend, veggies, and protein add-ins. The result is more flavourful than a traditional curry dish, as it’s more layered (so more depth). I am biassed, as many of our local Indian restaurants are similar to the British style takeout menus.

Stocks and gravies are a great way to make a traditional meat and potatoes style meal, or quick soup. They’re also great ways to fortify rice dishes, casseroles, and pan sauces.

### A theory for better cooking isn’t exactly new

The French have a long history of codifying and categorising food bases. Many of the French preparations can be adapted to freezer bases. A [demi-glace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demi-glace), for example,  freezes well and packs a bunch of flavour and texture, and can be adapted to dozens of different dishes. As I continue to learn to cook, I look to other cuisines and their history of sauces, bases, pastes, and pickled ingredients.

I enjoy cooking on my own terms, so I like to weaponize my love of cooking occasionally with my laziness, which is why I spent a lot of time filling my freezer with things that set up my week up for delicious food.
