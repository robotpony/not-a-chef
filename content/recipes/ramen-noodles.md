This is a simple chewy noodle recipe perfect for ramen, based on the [Serious Eats base recipe](https://www.seriouseats.com/homemade-ramen-noodles) with some tweaks from [Ramen Lord’s book](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1qLPoLxek3WLQJDtU6i3300_0nNioqeYXi7vESrtNvjQ/edit). These noodles will start out very shaggy, but will come together after resting.

Scale the ingredients by weight (using the percentages below) if you need more or less noodle.

## Mechanic

**Sodium carbonate** (baked baking soda) makes the dough alkaline, which is what gives ramen noodles their characteristic chew, yellow tint, and springiness, as opposed to a soft Italian-style pasta. At **38% hydration** this is a stiff, shaggy dough that only comes together with rest and lamination, not extra water.

## Ingredients

- 380g AP flour
- 20g vital wheat gluten
- 4g salt (1%)
- 6g sodium carbonate (1.5%), baked baking soda
- 145g water (38%) — consider testing some egg white to replace some of the water

Note: sodium carbonate can cause burns, so keep it out of your eyes and wash up after working with it.

## Method

1. Combine the flour and gluten in a large bowl. Dissolve the salt and sodium carbonate in the water.
2. Make a well in the middle of the flour and add the water mixture. Stir together with a fork until things start to stick, then blend with a pie dough blender or cutter, then with your hands.
3. Spread the shaggy dough on plastic wrap, press it into a wide, flat “board”, and wrap tightly. Rest in the fridge for 24 hours, or on the counter for 20+ minutes.
4. Cut the dough into 4 portions and use a rolling pin to flatten each enough to fit in your pasta maker. Carefully fold and repeat 2-3 times, focusing on laminating in one direction to encourage long gluten strand development (more [here](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1qLPoLxek3WLQJDtU6i3300_0nNioqeYXi7vESrtNvjQ/edit#)). Let rest again, wrapped tightly.
5. Work the dough further, down to the second-narrowest setting on your pasta maker. Switch to a cutting blade and run it through to noodlize it.
6. Toss the noodles in flour (or starch) and store in sealed deli containers or similar. Freezes well, lasts at least a few days in the fridge.

These noodles cook quickly, even from frozen (so no more than a few minutes).
