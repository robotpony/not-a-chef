## Mechanic

Bhajis are a spiced onion fritter bound with egg and gram flour. The potato adds body and absorbs some oil. **Keep the batter loose rather than thick**: tight batter produces stodgy centres. The frying oil does double duty: spent oil is fragrant and useful.

## Ingredients

- 4 white onions, halved and thinly sliced
- 1 medium potato, julienned
- 2 green chillies, finely sliced (or ½ teaspoon hot chilli powder)
- 2 teaspoons kosher salt
- 1 tablespoon curry powder or garam masala
- 1 teaspoon turmeric
- ½ teaspoon cumin
- 1 teaspoon ground coriander
- 1 teaspoon panch phoron (or ½ teaspoon ground fennel + ½ teaspoon ground fenugreek)
- 1 teaspoon dried fenugreek leaves (kasuri methi)
- handful fresh cilantro leaves, roughly chopped
- 3 eggs
- 185g gram (garbanzo bean) flour (~1½ cups), plus up to ½ cup more if needed

Neutral oil for frying.

## Method

Combine onion, potato, and chillies in a large bowl. Add spices and salt; mix thoroughly by hand. The salt will draw some moisture from the onion.

Add eggs and combine. Add gram flour and mix until a rough batter forms: it should hold together when you squeeze a handful, but not be stiff. Add up to 50g more flour if it feels too loose.

Heat neutral oil to 180°C (350°F) in a wide, heavy pot. Use enough oil to deep-fry.

Form golf-ball-sized fritters with damp hands. Drop in batches without crowding. Fry, turning occasionally, until deep golden brown all over, 4–5 minutes per batch.

Drain on a rack or paper towels.

## To serve

With green chutney or tamarind sauce while hot. Also good alongside a simple dal.

## Notes

- First test: batch was about 2x too large, but flavour was good. Size recipe down or plan to feed a crowd.
- Too watery on first attempt: squeeze excess moisture from the onions after salting if the batter looks loose.
- Fresh oil is important; old oil can foam aggressively.
