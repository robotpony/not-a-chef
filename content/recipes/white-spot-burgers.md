This Bruce’s take on the classic White Spot triple “O” deluxe burger (lettuce/tomatoes make it deluxe, cheddar and bacon make it a “BC” burger).

The Whitespot burgers are something like a 70:30 beef blend on a soft bun. I believe they use preformed patties these days, but I’ve found that at home a smashed 4-5oz patty works better as you can get a nice crust on them.

The sauces and formation of these burgers has changed a lot over the last 50 years, but if you turn back time they were a drive-in burger based on readily available ingredients. The triple “O” refers to the old drive-in order pads that had a list of checked “O”s for each condiment on the burger. These days they use 2 sauces, a red sauce (something relishy) and a white sauce (enriched, sweet mayo).

#### Main ingredients:

- Toasted buns (brioche, soft kaiser)
- Loosely shredded iceberg lettuce
- Sliced tomatoes (something big, red, and fresh)
- Kosher garlic pickles (long slice)
- Cheddar cheese sliced (real if you have it)

#### Beef patties

- 1 package of good, fresh hamburger (medium grind), 70:30 (regular)
- salt, pepper, and accent to taste
- Fat or oil (for the pan)

Split beef into 4-5 ounce chunks and form into loose meat balls.  Don’t over-pack the meat balls as you want some irregularity in the patty you will smash on the flat top or cast iron pan later (helps to form a better crust). Set aside while you prepare your other ingredients. Save seasoning until cook time.

#### Triple “O” (red) sauce

I’ve read that triple “O” sauce refers to the three sauce choices on the early ordering sheets (each “O” being where you’d mark which sauces the customer wanted). My best guess is that Tripple “O” is an enriched mayo, and a relish / chili sauce. In recent samples from White Spot, the red sauce and mayo are applied separately (but there is only one visible red sauce).

The red sauce on the White Spot burger has likely changed a lot over the years, but there are stories that it was produced by a local Vancouver supplier from simply repackaged bulk and readily available ingredients. This is typical of an evolution of an earlier in-house secret sauce made from mostly off the shelf ingredients.

- 2 parts Bick’s Red Relish
- 3 parts Heinz Chilli Sauce

Combine with a spoon. In home taste tests, this is close enough to work well.

#### White Spot mayo

Makes enough for about 8 burgers. Simply combine ingredients thoroughly.

- 1.5 cups Hellmann’s real mayo
- 1 egg yolk
- 1/2 tsp smoked paprika
- 3/4 tbsp (or one “blop”) of honey
- 1 tsp cider vinegar or lemon juice

White Spot’s mayo is a bit sweeter than normal, has fine flecks of red, and seems to have a yellow tinge to it. My guess is that it’s a more 50s style mayonnaise, which typically was more enriched and flavourful than today’s manufactured mayos.

### Directions:

1. Make the sweet mayo, triple “O” sauce, and get your other ingredients together.
2. Preheat a large, thick pan (on med/low), which you will turn up to med/high shortly before cooking.
3. Toast the buns in a medium-heat teflon pan and turn up your grilling pan (medium-high).
4. Add oil to the grilling pan, and drop in a meatball. Press the meat ball with a spatula (or smash press if you have one) until it’s ⅛ - ¼ inch thick. Do this as quickly as you can, and then leave the patty alone until you see liquid form on the top. Repeat for each patty, making sure the pan does not cool down.  
    Hint: some parchment paper can help prevent the patty from sticking to the spatula
5. Flip each patty once a crust has formed. Add cheese shortly before removing from the pan.
6. Assemble burgers.

The traditional deluxe triple “O” burger goes something like:

Bun

   Sweet mayo

   Lettuce

   Fresh tomato slice (or two)

   Kosher pickle (or three)

   Cheese

   Patty

   Triple “O”

Bun

For authenticity, lay the pickle over the bun and wrap.

Serve with salad or fresh cut fries!
