We discovered this dipping sauce on our travels recently: it adds a subtle, smoky depth to any sushi, and can be used over rice.
## Mechanic

The base is **5 parts soy sauce to 3 parts [[Mirin|mirin]]**. Boil the mirin first to cook off its alcohol, then only warm the soy; a hard boil dulls its flavour. The [[Katsuobushi|bonito]] steeps off the heat, like tea: a short steep gives a light, savoury sauce, and a full day gives a deeper smokiness.

## Ingredients

- 600 ml (2½ cups) mirin
- 1 tbsp sake, optional, for extra depth
- 1 L (4 cups) Japanese soy sauce (shoyu)
- 10–15 g (a large handful) dried bonito flakes (katsuobushi)

## Method

Pour the mirin and sake into a saucepan. Bring to a light boil over medium-high heat and let it bubble for 15 to 30 seconds to cook off the alcohol.

Lower the heat and pour in the soy sauce. Warm it gently until it almost reaches a simmer, without letting it boil.

Take the pan off the heat. Add the bonito flakes, cover, and let steep for 10 to 30 minutes. For a deeper, smokier sauce, leave it covered for a full day, in the fridge or at room temperature.

Strain through a fine-mesh sieve lined with cheesecloth or a paper coffee filter into a clean glass bottle or jar. Discard the flakes.

Seal and refrigerate.

## To serve

As a dipping sauce for sushi and sashimi, with [[Pickled ginger (gari)]] on the side.

## Notes

- Keeps in the fridge for up to 2 weeks.
- Close to nikiri (the sweetened soy sauce brushed onto sushi) and tosa [[Shoyu|shoyu]] (soy sauce seasoned with bonito).
- Halve the recipe for a smaller bottle; the ratio holds.
