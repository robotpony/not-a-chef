This is a quick and effective way to marinate chicken and pork. The mayo ensures spices don't burn, and it adheres to the cut surprisingly well.

## Mechanic

**Mayonnaise** coats the meat evenly and holds the seasonings in contact throughout marinating, producing more consistent browning and a tender surface. The big win is surface adhesion: the fat carries the spices and shields them, so they're less prone to burning.

## Ingredients

For ~2 butterflied chicken breasts.

- 40g mayonnaise (Hellmann's or Kewpie)
- zest of 1 lemon (or a squeeze of lemon juice)
- 2 cloves garlic (10g minced)
- ~1g black pepper (10 cranks)
- 1 teaspoon dried oregano
- 3g kosher salt
- 1g MSG

## Method

Remove fat, skin, and butterfly larger chicken breasts or pork chops. Pound out slightly, to a desirable shape.

Combine all ingredients and mix well. Coat chicken and marinate for 30 minutes minimum, up to 8 hours in the fridge.

Grill, broil, or pan-fry as usual. The mayo coating browns and crisps on contact with heat. Cook pork until the centre reads 63°C (145°F), then rest 5 minutes. Cook chicken until the centre reads 68°C (154°F); for a thin, butterflied breast the last minutes of cooking count toward the safety hold (see [[Food safety: time and temperature]]). Rest briefly.

## To serve

Serve chicken or pork with rice, a salad, or on a bun.

## Variations

- Teriyaki (sake, mirin, soy, ginger, sesame seeds, and sriracha for some spice)

## Notes

- I was skeptical about this technique until Kenji explained that mayo is oil emulsified with egg and acid, already seasoned with salt.
- Tested as a pure marinade (not salted separately first). Worked well; slightly salty, very tasty.
- Reduce salt if using a thinner cut that will marinate longer.
- Kewpie is a thick, savoury, enriched Japanese mayonnaise (packaged in a classic squeeze bottle).
- Cook chicken to 74°C (165°F) for vulnerable guests.
