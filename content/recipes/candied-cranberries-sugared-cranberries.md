I had attended a Roller Derby event that had a potluck and someone brought candied cranberries. They were surprisingly really good: tart and sweet, look like they are frosted in snow, and make a cool popping sound when you bite into them just right! Great for on a charcuterie board, a dessert topping, or just in a dish to add colour to a table or buffet. A teammate, Lashes, found the recipe on Instagram (@ourbestbites) and forwarded it to me, as we both enjoyed them at the event.

## Ingredients

- 1 bag Fresh Cranberries (12oz bag)
- Granulated Sugar
- Water

## Method:

1. Wash the cranberries and pick through them to remove any damaged or squishy ones.
2. Make a simple syrup with sugar and water: combine equal amount sugar and water in a saucepan and bring to a boil, stirring until the sugar has dissolved.
3. Once the simple syrup has cooled to room temperature, pour it over the berries and let them sit in the fridge overnight. This helps the sugar syrup thicken a bit and the sugar to stick better. If you are in a time crunch, you can move onto the next step, but they will be slightly more tart.
4. Use a slotted spoon to remove the berries from the syrup and set them on a cooling rack (make sure the rack is sitting over foil so the sugary syrup can drip off).
5. Once they have dried for 45 minutes to an hour, they should have just a thin coating of tacky syrup.
6. Roll the berries in sugar. The best combination is regular granulated sugar and also a coarse, larger grain sugar. Roll first in the coarse sugar and then in regular sugar to fill in the gaps.
7. Spread the berries out on a baking sheet and let dry completely (a couple hours or overnight). The result: incredible little sparkly berries. The outsides are crisp and then they burst in your mouth with a crunchy sugary exterior that balances out the tart berries.

These are best served the same day. If you need to store them, leave them at room temperature uncovered. But if it’s a humid environment, then you may want to put them in an airtight container.

If the cranberries look wet at all when you go to serve them after storing, give them a quick roll in sugar before serving.
