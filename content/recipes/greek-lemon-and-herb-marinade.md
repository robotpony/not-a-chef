This marinade is great for rice, tofu, chicken, shrimp, and fish. It’s a bright, herbal blend that balances acid, salt, and sweet. In a pinch it can be used for plating and on salads (where it’s even better blended).

#### Ingredients:

Note: amounts listed are estimates. I use what I have on hand and balance the tart/sweet/salt in the end. Some lemons require more sugar to balance. Some garlic is more powerful than others.

- 3-4 cloves of garlic finely minced
- 1 finely diced shallot (or onion)
- 4 tbsp of Greek oregano (freeze-dried or fresh)
- 1 cup of parsley, minced
- 4 tbsp of celery leaves minced (optional, and I usually have some in the fridge)
- 1-2 tbsp crushed (or minced) rosemary
- 1 tsp sage finely minced (or sub with poultry spice)
- 1 tsp (4g) fresh ground black pepper
- 2-3 tsp sugar (or honey)
- 4-5 tsp of kosher or sea salt
- 1 tsp accent (MSG)
- 1 tsp of roasted and granulated garlic (or garlic powder, but not garlic salt)
- 1 tsp ground coriander
- 2 lemons juiced (about 250ml)
- ½ cup olive oil

Makes 300-400ml of marinade.

### Directions:

1. Grind rosemary, pepper, and salt together in a mortar and pestle. Add sage, garlic and onion, and grind into a loose paste.
2. In a bowl, add spice paste and remaining unground spices, herbs, and MSG. Add lemon juice, olive oil, and tiny whisk until combined.
3. Taste and adjust pepper, salt, and sugar.

### Notes:

- The marinade should taste balanced. Tart, salty, sweet, and with a good kick of garlic and hint of onion. The herbs should work well together, but not overpower. The flavour should linger in a good way.
- Can be used as a marinade on most proteins, but the herbs need to be cooked gently (so slowly). Take care not to over marinate as well, as the acid can denature proteins and change their texture.
- Also great for roast potatoes, rice, and for making greek-fried rice.
