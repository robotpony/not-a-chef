## Mechanic

Thin slices and a hot pickle do the work. Shave the ginger **0.5 mm (1/64 in), never more than 1 mm**, so it turns tender instead of fibrous. A one-minute blanch and the hot amazu poured over both soften the ginger's bite. Young ginger has pink tips that tint the whole jar pale pink in the vinegar; mature ginger stays yellow. The amazu is **equal parts konbu dashi and rice vinegar**, sweetened with 5 tbsp sugar per 240 ml (1 cup) of liquid.

## Ginger

- 300 g (10.5 oz) young ginger
- a pinch of salt, for the blanching water

## Amazu

- 120 ml (½ cup) konbu dashi
- 120 ml (½ cup) rice vinegar
- 5 tbsp sugar
- ½ tsp salt

## Method

Break the ginger into small rounds that are easy to slice, and trim off any pointy nubs. Scrape off the thin skin with the spine of a knife or a spoon. Soak the pieces in a bowl of water for about 15 minutes.

Slice each piece as thin as you can along the fibre, ideally 0.5 mm (1/64 in) and no thicker than 1 mm (1/32 in). A mandoline makes this easier.

Bring a pot of water to a boil with the pinch of salt. Add the ginger and boil for 1 minute. Drain and let cool. Squeeze the slices hard between your palms to press out the water, then pack them into a jar or airtight container.

Combine the amazu ingredients in a small pot and heat, stirring, until the sugar and salt dissolve. Take it off the heat just before it boils; a long boil cooks off the vinegar's sharpness. Pour the hot amazu over the ginger.

Let cool, then refrigerate for a day before eating.

## To serve

With sushi, or alongside rice bowls and grilled fish.

## Notes

- Apple cider or white wine vinegar can stand in for rice vinegar.
- With mature ginger, peel off the tougher skin and slice across the fibre instead of along it.
- Konbu dashi is a Japanese stock made by soaking konbu (kelp) in water for at least 3 hours.
