Ahi folded through a spicy mayo, usually finished with tobiko or masago. It's rich and creamy, and the heat is the point. It's still an island poke, sold at the fish counter beside the shoyu. The mayo is usually folded through the fish; drizzled over a bowl, it's a second sauce rather than the seasoning.

## Picks

Creamy, rich, and hot.

- **The fish:** Ahi
- **The seasoning:** Spicy mayo, 3:1 Kewpie to sriracha, with a few drops of lime juice
- **Onions, seaweed, and crunch:** Green onion, and tobiko or masago on top
- **How it's served:** On its own, or over hot rice

## Where it comes from

Sriracha and mayonnaise are the base of the popular spicy ahi ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poke_(dish))).

## Why these picks

- **Kewpie.** It's made with yolks only and a little rice vinegar, so it's richer and tangier than North American mayo.
- **Lime.** It cuts the fat, so the fish still tastes like fish.
- **Roe.** Small bursts of salt against the cream.

## How it fails

- **Heavy:** too much mayo. Coat the fish; don't bury it.
- **One-note:** no acid. Add the lime.
