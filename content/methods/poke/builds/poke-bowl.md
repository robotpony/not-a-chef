The poke bowl that took over North America: a bowl of rice, the fish on top, and as many toppings again, with sauce over everything. It's a meal, and the fish is a third of it, so the rice and toppings need seasoning of their own.

## Picks

Fresh, filling, and built to order.

- **The fish:** [[Ahi]] or salmon
- **The seasoning:** [[Shoyu]] and sesame oil on the fish, and spicy mayo over the bowl
- **Onions, seaweed, and crunch:** Green onion, sesame seeds, and [[Furikake|furikake]]
- **How it's served:** A bowl of seasoned rice, about 1:1:1 rice, fish, and toppings: avocado, cucumber, edamame, pickled ginger

## Where it comes from

Poke became popular across North America from 2012, and the number of Hawaiian restaurants serving it doubled between 2014 and 2016. Most poke bowls outside Hawaii are closer to Korean hoe-deopbap, a raw-fish rice bowl, than to Hawaiian poke ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poke_(dish))).

| Constraint | Pick it explains |
|---|---|
| Sold as a lunch meal | Rice and toppings, not just fish |
| Built to order at a counter | A choice of fish, sauces, and toppings |

## Why these picks

- **Equal parts.** Enough rice to fill you, enough fish to taste it, and enough toppings for crunch and freshness in every bite.
- **Two sauces.** The shoyu seasons the fish; the sauce on top seasons the rest of the bowl.
- **Avocado and cucumber.** One creamy, one crisp.

## How it fails

- **Bland rice:** unseasoned and under-sauced. Season the rice as for sushi, or sauce the whole bowl.
- **Too much going on:** more than four or five toppings and the fish gets lost.
