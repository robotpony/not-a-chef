The poke most people mean. [[Ahi]] tossed in soy sauce and toasted sesame oil, with sweet onion, green onion, and toasted sesame seeds. It's savoury, a little nutty, and quick.

## Picks

Savoury, nutty, and fresh.

- **The fish:** Ahi
- **The seasoning:** [[Shoyu]] and sesame oil, 2:1, with a little rice vinegar and ginger
- **Onions, seaweed, and crunch:** Sweet Maui onion, green onion, and toasted sesame seeds
- **How it's served:** On its own, or over hot rice

## Where it comes from

Chinese and Japanese plantation workers brought soy sauce and sesame oil to Hawaii from the mid-1800s. Food historian Rachel Laudan dates poke's present form to around the 1970s, and today shoyu and sesame oil is its most common flavour ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poke_(dish))).

## Why these picks

- **2:1 shoyu to sesame oil.** Soy brings the salt and savouriness; the oil coats the fish and carries the nutty flavour. More oil and it's greasy.
- **Sweet onion.** Crunch, and a sweetness that balances the soy.

## How it fails

- **Too salty, dark, and firm:** it sat in the shoyu too long. Give it 15 minutes to an hour.
- **Greasy:** too much sesame oil. Keep it to half the soy.

## Variations

- **Wasabi poke:** wasabi stirred into the shoyu.
- **Garlic shoyu poke:** garlic oil in place of some of the sesame oil, with fresh local chili.
- **With [[Limu|limu]]:** chopped ogo through it, halfway back to [[Hawaiian limu poke]].
