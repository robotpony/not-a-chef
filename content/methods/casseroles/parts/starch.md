The starch is what makes a casserole a meal, and what makes a little filling go a long way. Where it goes matters more than which one it is. On top it's a lid that browns and keeps the filling moist. Underneath it's a bed that soaks up the sauce. Mixed through, every bite has some of everything.

## The variables

- **Position.** Top, bottom, or through. Mash and stuffing go on top; rice and noodles go underneath; pasta goes through.
- **Doneness.** Starch keeps cooking in the oven and drinks the sauce. Cooked pasta should go in a minute or two short of [[Al dente|al dente]]. Leftover rice and noodles are already done, so they need extra sauce rather than more time.
- **Amount.** About as much starch as filling, by volume. Less and it's a stew with a crust; more and it's dry.
- **Pasta size.** Small shapes (macaroni, penne) or cut-up long pasta, so it mixes with the sauce. Sheets get layered, and that's lasagne.
- **Dryness.** Mash for a topping should be stiff enough to hold a fork's ridges. Wet mash slumps into the filling and won't brown.

## Options

| Option | Pick it when | Changes |
|---|---|---|
| Mashed potatoes, on top | The filling is in gravy (a pie) | The top: ridged with a fork, browned under the broiler |
| Stuffing, on top or as a middle layer | It's holiday leftovers | The top: crisps on its own, needs no cheese |
| Rice or noodles, underneath | It's takeout or curry leftovers | The sauce: add extra, the starch soaks it up |
| Pasta, mixed through | You want a baked pasta | The sauce: loose, since pasta drinks it; cheese on top |
| Cornmeal or tortillas | It's a tamale pie or King Ranch chicken | The top: a cornmeal crust, or cheese over the tortillas |

## How it fails

- **Dry and stodgy:** too much starch, or leftover rice with no extra sauce.
- **A soggy lid:** the mash was too wet, or it went on a filling that was too thin.
