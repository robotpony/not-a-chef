Minced beef in gravy under mashed potatoes, the oldest casserole here. Ours is the weeknight version: [[Beef base]] from the freezer, a bag of frozen vegetables, and stock thickened into gravy in the same pan, so the only real cooking is the potatoes.

## Picks

Savoury and rich, under a soft, browned lid.

- **The starch:** Mashed potatoes on top, stiff enough to ridge
- **The filling:** Beef base and frozen peas, corn, and carrots
- **The sauce:** Stock thickened into a beef gravy in the pan
- **The top and the bake:** Mash ridged with a fork and a little cheddar, browned under the broiler

## Where it comes from

"Cottage pie" is first recorded in 1791, and "shepherd's pie" in the 19th century; the two names have been used for the same dish since ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shepherd%27s_pie)). Before refrigeration, a Sunday roast was cooked to last the week, and what was left of it was minced and baked under potato.

| Constraint | Pick it explains |
|---|---|
| Leftover roast, and no fridge to keep it | Cooked meat, minced and stretched in gravy |
| Potatoes are cheap and filling | A thick lid of mash |
| One oven, one dish | Baked, not served in parts |

Some say cottage pie is beef and shepherd's pie is lamb, but that distinction came later; in Canada either name can mean any meat under mash.

## Why these picks

- **Beef base.** It's already [[Braise|braised]] and seasoned, so the filling is ready in the time the potatoes take.
- **Frozen vegetables.** They go in frozen, cook in the bake, and stretch the beef.
- **Steamed potatoes.** Drier than boiled, so the mash holds its ridges and browns.

## How it fails

- **The mash sinks:** the filling was too thin. Simmer it until thick before it goes in the dish.
- **Pale top:** no broiler. The ridges are what brown.

## Variations

- **Shepherd's pie:** lamb in place of the beef.
- **Vegetarian:** a lentil base, or [[Canadian chili|chili]], in place of the beef.
