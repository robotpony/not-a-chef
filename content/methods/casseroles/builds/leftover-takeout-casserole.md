Family-style takeout leaves a little of everything: rice, noodles, two curries, some stir-fry. Piled into one dish and baked, it's somehow better than the meal it came from. The sauces soak into the rice, the edges crisp, and the flavours run together.

This one is upside down. The starch is the bed, not the lid, because the takeout dishes bring the sauce and the rice is there to catch it.

## Picks

Saucy, spicy, and mixed, over a crisp-edged bed of rice.

- **The starch:** Leftover rice or noodles, on the bottom
- **The filling:** The takeout dishes, piled on top
- **The sauce:** The dishes' own sauces, with a splash of stock or water
- **The top and the bake:** Nothing, or a little cheese; bake until the edges crisp

```stack
curry   | Curries and saucy dishes | sauce, spice
meat-chunks | Stir-fries and meat dishes | savoury
rice    | Rice or noodles          | bed, soaks the sauce

```

## Why these picks

- **The starch underneath.** Leftover rice is dry, and the sauces from above rehydrate it. On top it would just dry out more.
- **Saucy dishes on top.** They run down through the rest; dry dishes go in the middle.
- **One cuisine at a time.** Thai with Thai, Indian with Indian. Mixing them works less often than you'd think.

## How it fails

- **Dry, crunchy rice:** not enough sauce. Splash stock or coconut milk over the rice before the rest goes on.
- **Soggy:** too much sauce over noodles. Noodles drink less than rice.

## Variations

- **Cheese:** a little mozzarella over Indian leftovers, like a cheese naan.
- **A fried egg on top**, once it's out of the oven.
