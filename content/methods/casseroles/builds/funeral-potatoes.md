Potatoes in a rich sauce of cream soup, sour cream, and cheese, under a crunchy top of buttered cornflakes. It's the casserole as community care: the dish brought to a family, or served at the reception, after a funeral.

## Picks

Rich, cheesy, and crunchy on top.

- **The starch:** Hash browns or cubed potatoes, mixed through
- **The filling:** Onion; sometimes ham, peas, or broccoli
- **The sauce:** Cream soup or a cream sauce, with sour cream, butter, and cheddar
- **The top and the bake:** Buttered cornflakes, crushed crackers, or potato chips

## Where it comes from

It came out of Utah's Mormon community in the late 19th century, and recipes are in Relief Society cookbooks from the early 20th. By the mid-century, they called for convenience foods like canned soup and frozen hash browns ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funeral_potatoes)).
