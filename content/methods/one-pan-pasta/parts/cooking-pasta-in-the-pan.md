The pasta cooks in the pan it'll be sauced in, in just enough salted water to cover it. That's the whole trick. A big pot of water dilutes the starch the pasta gives off; a pan with a little water keeps it, and the starch is what tightens the sauce later.

## The variables

- **Pan.** Wide and straight-sided, so long pasta lies flat and the water stays shallow.
- **Water.** Just enough to cover the pasta. Add a splash more if it runs low before the pasta is close. Remove water using a ladle if it's not boiling off quickly enough.
- **Salt.** Salt the water as you would a pot. It reduces as it cooks, so go a little lighter.
- **Timing.** The pasta comes out 2–3 minutes short of the package time, because it finishes in the sauce (see [[Finishing pasta in the sauce]]).

## Options

| Option                     | Pick it when                     | Changes                                              |
| -------------------------- | -------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Short (penne, rigatoni)    | A thick sauce, like a meat sauce | Stir now and then so it doesn't stick                |
| Long (spaghetti, linguine) | A looser, oilier sauce           | Lay it flat and stir regularly.                      |
| Fresh                      | You have it, or made it          | Skip this step; it cooks in the sauce in 2–3 minutes |
## Notes

- Getting the ratio perfect takes some practice, but adding or removing water is trivial. I often remove ¼ - ½ cup of water.
- Add cheese off the heat. This prevents it from splitting, and saves a bunch of cleaning work.

## How it fails

- **Thin sauce:** too much water, so the starch was diluted. Boil it down before the sauce goes in. Thicken with cheese. Cheese is almost always the answer.
