A chicken sandwich follows the same rule as a burger: the patty brings salt, fat, and crunch, and the build adds something cold and crunchy and something sharp. The difference is the crust. A fried crust goes soft under anything wet, so the build keeps the juicy layers away from it, or puts a barrier between them.

## The variables

- **The bun.** Toasted, cut side down in a dry pan. A soft hamburger bun, a long sesame bun for the BK classic, or a kaiser for a breast with some chew. Toasting in olive oil suits the Italian build.
- **Mayo.** On both buns. It's the fat that a lean chicken patty lacks, and it keeps the bun from soaking up juice.
- **Cold crunch.** Shredded iceberg (shredduce), slaw, or pickles.
- **Sharp.** Thin raw red onion, mustard, or sour pickles ([[Sandwich sour pickles]]).
- **Sweet.** Sweet pickles, maple, or honey. Chicken takes more sweetness than beef does.
- **Juicy.** Tomato, sliced thin, kept off the crust.

## Order

Bottom up:

1. Bottom bun, mayo
2. Patty, with any sauce, mustard, or cheese on top of it
3. Pickles, onion
4. Tomato, lettuce or slaw
5. Mayo on the top bun, then the top bun

Wet things go above the patty, not under it, so the bottom crust stays dry against the mayo.

## How it fails

- **One note:** no cold crunch or nothing sharp. The [[The impossible quest for the perfect chicken burger|McChicken problem]].
- **Soggy crust:** tomato, slaw, or sauce sat on it too long. Build and eat it.
- **Dry:** no mayo. Chicken needs the fat.
