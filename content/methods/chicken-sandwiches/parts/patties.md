A chicken patty is a whole breast pounded thin or ground chicken pressed into a round, and either way it needs salt inside before it cooks. Breast is lean and bland, and it dries out fast. Salt gets it seasoned through and holds onto its moisture. Unlike beef, salt in chicken is the point: a [[Brine|brine]] keeps a whole cut tender, and salt worked into mince binds it into a patty that holds together in the fryer.

## The variables

- **The cut.** Whole breast for a sandwich you can tell was a piece of meat. Ground chicken (half breast, half thigh, or store-bought) for a round, even patty like the BK classic. Thigh works at every step and forgives more overcooking than breast.
- **Thickness.** Pound or butterfly a breast to an even 1 cm (⅜ in). Thinner pieces cook before the crust burns and take the brine faster.
- **Size.** About 15% larger than the bun for a breast, 1–2 cm wider for a ground patty. Both shrink in the pan.
- **Salt.** A whole cut is brined: buttermilk and salt for 30 minutes to 1 hour, or a vinegar brine for 30 minutes. Left overnight, a thin piece turns ham-like. Ground chicken gets about 1.25–1.5% salt by weight mixed in with a little water.
- **Baking soda.** A pinch in ground chicken (0.125–0.25%) raises the pH so the patty stays juicy through a hot fry. More of it, worked hard, turns the patty bouncy and sausage-like.

## Options

| Option | Pick it when | Changes |
|---|---|---|
| Whole breast, buttermilk soak | You want the crunchiest sandwich; the cornmeal crust | Breading: cornmeal [[Dredge|dredge]] |
| Ground chicken, lightly mixed | You want a round, even, fast-food-style patty | Breading: light dredge or double coat |
| Whole breast, [[Mayo marinade]] | A weeknight sandwich, no frying | Breading: none; pan-[[Sear|sear]] |
| Ground chicken, worked until tacky | You want a springy, sausage-like patty | Breading: optional |

The texture scale behind these options, from mega crunch to floppy, is in [[The impossible quest for the perfect chicken burger]].

## How it fails

- **Dry and stringy:** the breast was too thick, uneven, or not brined. Pound it even.
- **Ham-like:** a thin breast sat in the brine too long.
- **Bouncy, like a fish ball:** too much baking soda, or the mince was worked too long.
- **Falls apart in the fryer:** the ground chicken wasn't mixed enough with its salt to bind.
