The crust is what a chicken sandwich is about. It brings the crunch and most of the fat, so it has to stick, stay crisp under the toppings, and brown before a thin piece of chicken overcooks. Every coating follows the same order: something wet so it sticks, the dry coat, a rest so it sets, then a shallow fry.

## The variables

- **The wet layer.** Buttermilk from the soak, a loose cornstarch batter, or beaten egg. It's the glue.
- **The dry coat.** Flour sticks; cornmeal, breadcrumbs, or panko crunch. Most coatings mix one of each.
- **The rest.** 10–20 minutes after coating. The coat turns pasty and grips, and it stays on in the fat.
- **The fat.** About 5 mm (¼ in) of shortening or oil in a wide pan, at 175–190°C (350–375°F). Shortening holds its temperature better and gives a drier crust.
- **Done.** Deep golden on both sides and 70°C (160°F) in the centre, 3–4 minutes a side for a 1 cm patty.
- **Holding.** On a rack, never on paper towels, which steam the bottom crust soft.

## Options

| Option | Pick it when | How |
|---|---|---|
| Cornmeal dredge | You want a dark, gritty, mega crunch | Equal parts cornmeal and flour by volume, over the buttermilk soak (see [[Cornmeal fried chicken]]) |
| Double coat | A ground patty; you want layered crunch | Flour dredge, loose cornstarch batter, then breadcrumbs (see [[Pan-fried chicken burger]]) |
| Standard breading | A lighter, even crust | Flour, beaten egg, panko |
| None | A soft, floppy sandwich | Pan-sear a brined or mayo-marinated breast in a little oil |

## How it fails

- **The crust slides off:** no rest after coating, or the chicken was too wet or too dry when it went into the dredge.
- **Dark outside, raw middle:** the patty is too thick or the fat too hot. Pound it thinner.
- **Pale and greasy:** the fat was too cool, or too many patties went in at once.
- **Soft by the time it's eaten:** it sat on paper, or wet toppings sat on the crust (see [[Assembling chicken sandwiches]]).
