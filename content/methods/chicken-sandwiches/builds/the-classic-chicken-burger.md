The BK classic at home: a ground chicken patty, lightly breaded and fried, with mayo and lettuce. It's salt, pepper, and fat in the right proportions, and nothing else. It's the build that taught me a mediocre patty can still make a great sandwich.

## Picks

Salt, pepper, and fat, with cold lettuce.

- **Chicken sandwich patties:** Ground chicken, salted, with a pinch of baking soda
- **Breading and frying chicken patties:** Double coat or light breading, pan-fried
- **Assembling chicken sandwiches:** Shredded lettuce, on a toasted bun
- **Chicken sandwich sauces:** Mayo on both buns

## Where it comes from

Burger King's Chicken Specialty Sandwich (now the Original Chicken Sandwich) came out in 1979, a breaded white-meat patty on a long sesame bun with lettuce and mayo ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burger_King_Specialty_Sandwiches)). I ate a lot of them working at BK in the late 80s; the story is in [[The impossible quest for the perfect chicken burger]].

## Why these picks

- **Ground, not whole.** An even, round patty, juicy from the baking soda, that eats like the fast-food original.
- **Lots of mayo.** The patty is lean; the mayo is the sandwich's fat, and it soaks into the crust as it sits.
- **Pepper-forward.** Black pepper in the [[Dredge|dredge]] does most of the seasoning.

## How it fails

- **Dry:** too little mayo.
- **Bland:** under-salted patty, or no pepper in the dredge.

## Variations

- **Deluxe:** add tomato, pickles, and mustard. My order, Whopper style.
- **Long bun:** the BK shape. Form the patty oval to match.
