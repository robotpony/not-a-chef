A burger sauce adds sweetness, acid, and sharpness, and sometimes fat. There are two ways to get there: keep the condiments separate and let them meet on the burger, or blend them into one spread. Either way, the sauce sits against the bread, where it soaks in, and it shouldn't drown the onion and pickle.

## The variables

- **Sweet to sharp.** Ketchup brings sweetness; mustard brings sharpness. **5:1 ketchup to mustard** is the fast-food balance (see [[McDouble]]).
- **Fat.** Mayo carries a blended sauce and softens the mustard. Kewpie adds sweetness and acidity.
- **Pickle.** Diced pickle or relish gives a spread its texture and sourness.
- **Rest.** A blended spread with raw onion or pickle tastes better after an hour in the fridge, once the onion mellows.

## Options

| Option                        | Pick it when                    | Ratio                                                                                             |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Ketchup and mustard, separate | Deluxe and fast-food burgers    | 5:1 ketchup to mustard                                                                            |
| [[Bruce's burger sauce]]      | Swiss, mozza, or deluxe burgers | 1:1:1 mayo, diced pickle, mustard                                                                 |
| [[In-N-Out burger spread]]    | Diner-style griddle burgers     | Mayo-based, with ketchup, relish, and a little vinegar and sugar                                  |

## Condiments

The jarred and bottled condiments the builds use, on their own or as the base of a sauce.

| Condiment | What it brings | Used on |
|---|---|---|
| Ketchup | Sweet | [[McDouble burger]], [[Fast-food double burger]], [[Smash burger]], [[Teen burger]]; on the table for the [[Pub burger]] |
| Yellow mustard | Sharp | The same burgers as ketchup, and alone on the [[Grilled onion burger]] |
| Mayonnaise | Fat; carries a blended sauce | [[Pub burger]], [[Big Boy burger]]; the base of Triple “O” mayo, Mozza sauce, and Teen sauce |
| Red relish | Sweet, sour, texture | [[Big Boy burger]]; with chili sauce in Triple “O” sauce and Mozza sauce |
| Chili sauce | Sweet, tangy tomato | Triple “O” sauce ([[Drive-in deluxe burger]]) and Mozza sauce ([[Mozza burger]]) |
| Thousand Island | Sweet, creamy, tangy | [[Big Boy burger]], and a touch in Mozza sauce |
| Dill pickles | Sour, crunch | Most builds, sliced thin or lengthwise |
| Double-sour pickles | The strongest sour | [[Drive-in deluxe burger]] |

Double-sour pickles are hard to buy and easy to make: sprinkle citric acid over ordinary sandwich-style dill pickles and let them sit. They get remarkably close to commercial double sours. On a fast-food double, non-kosher dills with a little citric acid or vinegar do the same job (see [[McDouble]]).

## How it fails

- **Harsh:** too much mustard. A squeeze of ketchup or a splash of pickle brine softens it.
- **Too mild:** lean more pickle for brightness.
