This is the rich build. Where the drive-in deluxe is all contrast, this one leans into salt and fat: two thin, soft patties with a slice of processed cheese melted between them. Pickles, ketchup, mustard, and onion keep it from going heavy. It's the one case where American cheese earns its place on a burger.

Most every chain sells one; the [[McDouble burger]] is one chain's version. Some regions lean harder on mustard, which I prefer, and I like the sharper cut of real onion through the fat (see Variations).

## Picks

Salty, sweet, and rich; little crunch.

- **Forming burger patties:** Two thin patties, 75 g each, double-ground
- **Seasoning burgers:** 4:2:1 salt, MSG, and pepper, after cooking
- **Cooking patties:** From frozen on a hot griddle, cheese on after the flip
- **Assembling burgers:** Half a slice of processed cheese between the patties, thin pickles, rehydrated dried minced onion, on a small bun pressed a little flat
- **Sauces and condiments:** Ketchup and mustard, 5:1

## Where it comes from

The value-menu double of the 1990s: frozen, double-ground patties and fixed condiment ratios, so it tastes the same at every chain.

## Why these picks

- **Two thin patties, not one thick one.** Twice the crust, and the cheese melts between them.
- **Double-ground beef.** It gives the soft, almost pillowy texture of a fast-food patty instead of a coarse, chewy one.
- **Processed cheese.** It melts smooth into a thin patty. Use half a slice of Kraft Deluxe; a full one is too thick.
- **Rehydrated dried onion.** Dried minced onion, soaked in water, instead of raw. It's a clever ruse: the flavour is mellower than raw onion and distinctly different, and it's very obvious when you taste it.
- **5:1 ketchup to mustard.** The fast-food sweet-savoury balance.
- **Pickle and mustard.** The only sharp and sour on the burger, so they can't be skipped.

## How it fails

- **Heavy:** no pickle or mustard to cut the fat.
- **Coarse, chewy patty:** the beef was single-ground.

## Variations

- **Bacon double cheese:** add crisp bacon. Only if it's crisp; chewy bacon pulls the burger apart. Water-frying gives flatter, more evenly crisp strips (see [[Reverse cooking: reverse sear and water frying]]).
- **Fresh onion:** diced white onion, soaked in cold water and drained, instead of the dried. Sharper, and less like the chain.
- **Single:** one 45 g patty on a small bun, the drive-thru hamburger (see the [[McDouble]] variations).
