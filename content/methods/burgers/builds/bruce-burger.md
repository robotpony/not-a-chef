The burger I make at home. It starts from [[The Whopper]]: one wide patty with everything cold stacked on top. The technique comes from the [[White Spot burger]]: a thin griddled patty, seasoned after it cooks, built deluxe. Every part is there because I tested the alternative and it lost. The story of how it got here is in [[The quest for the perfect burger]].

## Picks

Sharp, sour, and crunchy over a loose, salty patty.

- **Forming burger patties:** One thin patty, 140 g, not mixed
- **Seasoning burgers:** Salt and cracked black pepper, after cooking
- **Cooking patties:** Griddled hot, flipped once
- **Assembling burgers:** Shredded lettuce, thin tomato, paper-thin raw purple onion, onion sautéed in the beef fat, double-sour pickles, on a toasted hamburger bun ([[Chef John's hamburger buns]]), wrapped in paper to rest
- **Sauces and condiments:** Ketchup and mustard, kept separate

```stack
bun-top          | Toasted bun                | soft, toasted
lettuce-shredded | Shredded lettuce           | cold crunch
tomato           | Thin tomato                | juicy, acid
ketchup          | Ketchup                    | sweet
onion-raw        | Raw purple onion           | sharp
pickle           | Double-sour pickles        | sour
onion-fried      | Onion sautéed in beef fat  | sweet
patty-thin       | Thin patty, 140 g          | salt, fat, savoury
mustard          | Mustard                    | sharp
bun-base         | Toasted bun                | barrier
```

## Why these picks

- **Not mixed, seasoned after.** Salt added early pulls moisture out and changes the texture before the patty hits the griddle. Season the raw patty and you get a denser, springier bite instead of a loose, crumbly one.
- **Griddled, not grilled.** The Whopper's shape with a flat-top crust instead of char, and the fat stays in the pan for the onions.
- **Two onions, paper thin.** Raw purple onion for sharpness, onion sautéed in the beef fat for sweetness. Together they do more than either alone.
- **Double-sour pickles.** Hard to buy and easy to make: sprinkle citric acid over ordinary sandwich-style dill pickles and let them sit.
- **Ketchup and mustard, apart.** Each keeps its own flavour instead of blending into one sauce.
- **Rest in paper.** A few minutes wrapped in burger paper steams the bun and settles the flavours. It tastes better than the same burger straight off the griddle.

## How it fails

- **Springy, dense patty:** it was mixed, or salted raw.
- **One-note:** the double-sour pickles or the raw onion are missing, so nothing cuts the fat.
- **Mostly bun, or mostly meat:** the patty and bun don't match in size.

## Variations

- **Blended sauce:** swap the ketchup and mustard for [[Bruce's burger sauce]] or the [[In-N-Out burger spread]].
- **Drive-in deluxe:** swap the ketchup and mustard for Triple “O” sauces (see [[Drive-in deluxe burger]]).
