The first double-decker. A quarter pound of beef is split into two thin patties, and a middle slice of bun gives each one its own deck of sauce and lettuce. It's the same weight of beef as a single burger, but twice as tall and with twice the crust. Cool lettuce and a sweet, creamy sauce on both decks balance the salt and fat.

## Picks

Tall, creamy, and sweet; soft crunch on every deck.

- **Forming burger patties:** Two thin patties, a quarter pound split in half, wider than the bun
- **Seasoning burgers:** Salt and pepper on the griddle
- **Cooking patties:** Griddled hot, flipped once, cheese on the lower patty after the flip
- **Assembling burgers:** A three-part sesame bun, toasted; shredded iceberg on both decks; one slice of American cheese
- **Sauces and condiments:** Mayonnaise and red relish, or a Thousand Island special sauce

## Where it comes from

Glendale, California. Bob Wian opened Bob's Pantry there in 1936, and in February 1937 a bass player from a band that ate there asked for something different. Wian made a double-decker, meant to look ridiculous, like a leaning tower. It was named for a boy who came in while Wian was looking for a name, whom he greeted with "Hello, Big Boy." The original was a quarter pound of fresh beef in two thin patties, with lettuce, American cheese, and mayonnaise with red relish (sweet pickle relish, ketchup, and chili sauce) on a three-layer sesame bun. In the late 1940s Wian licensed it to regional chains like Frisch's in Cincinnati, which sold it under their own restaurant names, and the sauce drifted: Frisch's used tartar sauce on a plain bun with pickles. ([Big Boy Restaurants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Boy_Restaurants), Wikipedia.)

| Constraint | Pick it explains |
|---|---|
| A regular asked for something new | A double-decker, built to look tall |
| A quarter pound of beef | Two thin patties, not one thick one |
| Franchised to regional chains under their own names | Red relish, Thousand Island, or tartar sauce, depending on where you ate it |

## Why these picks

- **Two thin patties.** Twice the browned surface from the same beef, and each cooks through in a couple of minutes on a griddle.
- **The middle bun.** It separates the decks, so each patty has its own sauce and lettuce, and it soaks up the juices that would otherwise make the bottom bun soggy.
- **Lettuce on both decks.** The only crunch, and the only thing cool on the burger.
- **Sweet, creamy sauce.** Relish and ketchup in mayonnaise, so the sweetness and the fat arrive together.

## How it fails

- **Leans or slides:** patties too thick, or too much sauce between the decks.
- **Soggy middle:** the middle bun wasn't toasted on both sides.
- **Bready:** the middle bun is too thick, so bun outweighs beef.
