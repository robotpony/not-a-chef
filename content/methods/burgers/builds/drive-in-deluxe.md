This is a variation of the [[White Spot burger]], which is a specific drive-in/diner style burger that I grew up with. I love onions, so we include 2 types here.

The build works because everything above the patty is cold, crunchy, sharp, or sour, and the patty below brings the salt, fat, and savoury depth. Every part is there because the alternative was tried and lost.

## Picks

Cold crunch and sharp against a salty patty. Often served as a sandwich slice over the bun, so it's optional.

- **Forming burger patties:** One thin patty, 140 g, not mixed
- **Seasoning burgers:** Salt and pepper after cooking
- **Cooking patties:** Griddled hot, flipped once
- **Assembling burgers:** Shredded lettuce, thin tomato, paper-thin raw purple onion, onion sautéed in the beef fat, double-sour pickles, cracked pepper, on a toasted bun
- **Sauces and condiments:** Triple “O”: red relish sauce on the base, sweet enriched mayo under the top bun

```stack
bun-top               | Toasted bun                   | soft, toasted
mayo                  | Sweet mayo                    | rich, sweet
lettuce-shredded      | Shredded lettuce              | cold crunch
tomato                | Thin tomato                   | juicy, acid
onion-raw             | Raw purple onion              | sharp
pickle                | Double-sour pickles           | sour
onion-fried           | Onion sautéed in beef fat     | sweet
patty-thin            | Thin patty, 140 g             | salt, fat, savoury
sauce-burger          | Red sauce                     | sweet, sharp
bun-base              | Toasted bun                   | barrier
```

## Where it comes from

This is the diner and drive-in burger of the 1960s, the burger they still make: built from ingredients a drive-in could buy reliably and cooked fast on a flat-top. Most of the picks are that kitchen's constraints.

| Constraint | Pick it explains |
|---|---|
| A flat-top griddle and fast service | A thin patty, smashed, flipped once |
| Ingredients bought in bulk | Bottled relish, chili sauce, and mayo; iceberg lettuce |
| Eaten in the car, from paper | A soft bun, wrapped so it steams |
| "Deluxe" as an add-on | Lettuce and tomato |

The Triple “O” name comes from the carhop order tickets: a customer who wanted extra sauce, relish, and mayonnaise got three “O”s marked on the slip, and the mark carried into White Spot's point-of-sale system as late as the 2010s. The sauces and builds have changed over the decades; [[White Spot burger]] has the closest home version of both sauces.

## Why these picks

- **Two onions.** Raw for sharpness, sautéed in the beef fat for sweetness. Together they do more than either alone.
- **Double-sour pickles.** The strongest sour on the burger. Make them with a sprinkle of citric acid powder over ordinary dill pickle chips (round slices).
- **Two sauces, apart.** The red sauce goes on the base, where it meets the hot patty; the mayo goes under the top bun with the cold layers. Each stays distinct instead of blending into one.
- **Rest in paper.** A few minutes wrapped in burger paper steams the bun and settles the flavours.

## How it fails

- **No cold crunch:** without the lettuce and pickle, it's a one-note burger.
- **No onion:** the onion is the most important part of a deluxe build.

## Variations

- **BC burger:** add cheddar and bacon (White Spot's name for it).
- **Ketchup and mustard:** swap the red sauce for ketchup and mustard, kept separate.
- **Blended sauce:** swap both sauces for [[Bruce's burger sauce]] or the [[In-N-Out burger spread]].
