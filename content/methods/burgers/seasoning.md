Salt only ever touches the outside of a burger patty. Mixed into raw ground meat, salt pulls out moisture and binds the proteins, the same way it does in sausage, and the patty comes out dense and springy instead of loose and crumbly. Season the surface, either as the patty hits the pan or as it comes off.

## The variables

- **When.** As the patty comes off the heat is the safest. Seasoning the top just before you smash it also works, since the salt stays on the surface and goes straight into the crust. Never before shaping.
- **What.** Salt and black pepper are the base, with MSG optional.
- **How much.** For a seasoning blend, mix by weight: **4:2:1 salt, MSG, and black pepper**, plus 0.25 parts onion powder if you like (from the [[McDouble]]).

## Options

| Option | Pick it when | Changes |
|---|---|---|
| Salt and pepper, after cooking | Any burger; the default | Nothing |
| Salt, pepper, and MSG (4:2:1) | You want the fast-food flavour | Nothing |
| Season the top, then smash | Smashed patties | Cooking: season in the pan, before the smash |

## How it fails

- **Springy, sausage-like patty:** salt went into the meat before shaping.
