The muesli that comes in a bag: raw flakes, dried fruit, and nuts, mixed and kept dry. Pour milk over it and give it a few minutes to soften. It's the simplest to make, since nothing is cooked.

## Picks

Soft, wholesome, and lightly sweet from the fruit.

- **Choosing the grains:** Rolled oats with wheat or barley flakes
- **Toasting or soaking:** Raw
- **Fruit, nuts, and seeds:** Classic, 3:1:1: raisins and chopped apricot, almonds or hazelnuts, sunflower seeds
- **Milk, yogurt, or juice:** Milk, left 5–10 minutes

## Where it comes from

Muesli spent its first half-century as hospital food: Bircher-Benner's dish was served to patients around 1900 (see [[Bircher muesli]]). It became a commercial product in Switzerland in 1959, sold dry in a bag, in raw and toasted versions. A dry mix can't hold fresh apple, so the fruit became dried fruit, and the soak became a few minutes in milk.

| Constraint | Pick it explains |
|---|---|
| Sold dry, kept on a shelf | Dried fruit in place of fresh apple |
| Eaten at home, in the morning | Milk poured over, not an overnight soak |

## Why these picks

- **Raw.** No oven, so it's made in the time it takes to stir it together, and it keeps for months.
- **Mixed flakes.** Raw oats on their own go soft all the way through; the firmer flakes keep some chew.
- **Waiting.** Ten minutes in milk takes the raw, floury edge off.

## How it fails

- **Floury and bland:** eaten straight after pouring. Give it the 10 minutes, or toast it (see [[Crunchy muesli]]).
- **All the fruit at the bottom:** the pieces were too big or too few. Chop to raisin size.

## Variations

- **Fruit and nut:** about 2:1:1, with dried apple, apricot, or papaya and more almonds or walnuts.
