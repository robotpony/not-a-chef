Granola is muesli baked with oil and a sweetener, stirred as it bakes, until it's crisp and clustered. It's here for the contrast. The oil and syrup are what make the clusters, and they're also what turn a breakfast cereal into something close to a dessert. The North American kind leans heavily on both.

## Picks

Sweet, rich, and clustered.

- **Choosing the grains:** Rolled oats, sometimes with puffed rice
- **Toasting or soaking:** Baked with oil and a sweetener (honey, maple syrup, or brown sugar)
- **Fruit, nuts, and seeds:** Nuts and seeds baked in; dried fruit, and often chocolate, added after
- **Milk, yogurt, or juice:** Milk or yogurt, or eaten dry by the handful

## Where it comes from

Dr. James Caleb Jackson made the first "Granula" at his sanitarium in Dansville, New York, in 1863, from Graham flour. John Harvey Kellogg made a similar cereal and renamed it granola to avoid legal trouble with Jackson. The name and the food came back in the 1960s, with fruit and nuts added, as a health food ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granola)).

## Why these picks

- **Oil and sweetener.** The syrup is the glue, and the oil carries the heat so it browns evenly. Together they make clusters.
- **Fruit after baking.** Dried fruit scorches in the oven.

## How it fails

- **No clusters:** too little syrup, or it was stirred late in the bake. Press it down and leave it alone for the last stretch.
- **Too sweet to be breakfast:** the usual problem. That's why the muesli I make is [[Crunchy muesli]].

## Variations

- **Less oil and sugar:** halve both. It clusters less, and it's on its way back to crunchy muesli.
- **Bars:** more binder, pressed into a pan: [[Granola bars]].
