The muesli I make. The grains are toasted dry in the oven, so they taste nutty and stay crisp in milk, but nothing coats them: no oil, no sugar. It has the tooth of granola without the fat and sweetness the North American versions lean on.

## Picks

Crunchy and nutty, sweet only from the fruit.

- **Choosing the grains:** Rolled oats and wheat flakes, and sometimes other flakes
- **Toasting or soaking:** Toasted dry, with a pinch of salt
- **Fruit, nuts, and seeds:** Toasted nuts and dried fruit, about 3:1:1, the fruit stirred in once the grain is cool
- **Milk, yogurt, or juice:** Milk or oat milk, eaten straight away; frozen berries on top

## Where it comes from

Toasted muesli was on Swiss shelves from 1959, alongside the raw kind (see [[Classic muesli]]). In North America it was on shelves as crunchy muesli by the 1980s at least.

## Why these picks

- **Dry toasting.** It browns the grain for flavour and drives off moisture for crunch. Oil would make it richer, but it doesn't need it.
- **No sugar.** The dried fruit sweetens it, and toasted grain tastes sweeter than raw.
- **Oats and wheat together.** They toast in about the same time, so they go on one pan.
- **Frozen berries.** They thaw into the milk and chill it, so the muesli stays crisp a little longer.

## How it fails

- **Soft in the jar:** it went in before it was cool. Let it cool on the pan.
- **Bitter or scorched:** the layer was thin at the edges, or the nuts were in for the whole time. Stir halfway, and give the nuts less time.
- **Bland:** under-toasted, or no salt. Take it to golden, not just dry.

## Variations

- **More fruit:** push it toward fruit and nut, about 2:1:1.
- **With yogurt:** a spoonful on top in place of some of the milk.
