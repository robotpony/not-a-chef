The first muesli, and the one that's mostly apple. Raw oats soak overnight, and in the morning a whole apple is grated straight into them, with lemon to keep it from browning. It's cold, wet, and fresh.

## Picks

Creamy, tart, and fresh, with a little sweetness.

- **Choosing the grains:** Rolled oats only
- **Toasting or soaking:** Soaked overnight
- **Fruit, nuts, and seeds:** A whole grated apple, skin and all, and a spoonful of chopped walnuts, almonds, or hazelnuts
- **Milk, yogurt, or juice:** Water or milk for the soak, then lemon juice and a spoonful of cream and honey (or sweetened condensed milk) in the morning

## Where it comes from

Swiss physician Maximilian Bircher-Benner created it around 1900 for the patients at his sanatorium in Zürich, where fresh fruit and vegetables were the centre of the treatment ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muesli)). It wasn't a breakfast cereal. He served it before meals, and called it simply "the dish." His recipe was 1 tablespoon of oats soaked for 12 hours in 3 tablespoons of water, then one large apple (or two or three small ones), grated whole, core and all, with the juice of half a lemon, a tablespoon of nuts, and a tablespoon of cream and honey or condensed milk.

| Constraint | Pick it explains |
|---|---|
| A diet built on raw fruit | More apple than oats |
| Raw oats, no cooking | The long soak |
| Grated apple browns fast | Lemon juice, and grating it straight into the bowl |

## Why these picks

- **Soaked oats.** Twelve hours in liquid softens raw oats as much as cooking would, without heat.
- **Grated apple.** It's the body of the dish, not a topping. Grated, it becomes part of the texture.
- **Lemon.** It stops the apple browning and keeps the whole bowl bright.

## How it fails

- **Gluey:** too little liquid, or quick oats. Use rolled oats and loosen it with milk in the morning.
- **Brown and flat:** the apple was grated ahead, or the lemon was left out.

## Variations

- **Overnight oats:** the modern version, sold as "overnight oats" since the 2010s. More oats, less apple, often soaked in yogurt and milk with berries on top.
- **Café Bircher:** apple or orange juice for the soak in place of lemon, with berries, pear, or vanilla.
