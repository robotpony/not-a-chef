My working-day tea: sweet, tart, and calming. A 2 L jug, brewed hot for the same day or cold for a smoother one a day or two later. For a single shaken glass, see [[Lemon-lime iced tea]].

## Ingredients

- 6 bags high-quality black tea, such as Ceylon
- 1 L boiled water
- 100 g (½ cup) granulated white sugar
- 1 lime, juiced
- 1 pinch of kosher salt
- about 1 L cold water or ice, to top up

## Method

**Steep the tea.** Pour the boiled water over the tea bags in a large heat-safe container (I use a 2.5 L Pyrex). Steep 4–5 minutes, but no longer, then lift out the bags with a spider or slotted spoon.

**Sweeten.** Stir in the sugar and salt until dissolved. Add the lime juice. Let cool to room temperature.

**Top up and chill.** Pour into a 2 L jug and top up with cold water or ice. Chill in the fridge for at least 1 hour.

## Variations

### Cold-brewed

Smoother and less bitter, with no kettle, but it takes a day or two. Put 6 bags Ceylon black tea in a 2 L glass or plastic jug with 2 L cold tap water, and steep in the fridge for 24–48 hours. Remove the bags, then stir in 100 g (½ cup) white sugar and 80 ml (⅓ cup) lime juice, lemon juice, or a mix until the sugar dissolves. Serve over ice.

Steep it in the fridge, not on the counter. Tea left at room temperature for hours is the "sun tea" pattern food safety agencies warn against: lukewarm tea is a good home for bacteria.

## Notes

- In the summer, swap one black tea bag for a peach or raspberry tea; peach from the farmers' market when I can find it.
