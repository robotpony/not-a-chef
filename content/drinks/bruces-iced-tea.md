My working-day tea, and my favourite spring and summer drink: sweet, tart, and calming. I shake it by the glass most days now; the jug versions are below.

## Mechanic

Brew the tea **at least double strength**, because shaking it with ice melts a good share of the ice into the glass. Normal-strength tea comes out thin and watery.

## Ingredients

#### Tea, for several glasses

- 6 bags strong black tea, such as Ceylon
  *or 3 heaping teaspoons of loose leaf tea (like breakfast tea)*
- 1 L boiled water

#### Per glass, 400–700 ml (16–20 oz)

- 1 - 2 tbsp sugar (3–5 heaping teaspoons)
- 1–2 squirts each fresh lemon and lime juice (10–25 ml in all)
- ice

## Method

**Brew it strong.** Pour the boiled water over the tea and steep 4–5 minutes, but no longer, then lift out the bags. Let cool. It keeps in the fridge for a few days.

**Shake each glass.** Put the sugar, lemon juice, and lime juice in a cocktail shaker (or a pint glass, with a second one to cap it) with ice. Fill with tea and shake until cold and slightly frothy.

**Pour.** Strain over fresh ice in a tall glass.

## To serve

Straight. Start at the low end of the sugar and citrus and adjust the next glass.

## Variations

### Hot-brewed jug

A 2 L jug for the same day, already sweetened. Brew the tea as above in a large heat-safe container (I use a 2.5 L Pyrex). Stir in 100 g (½ cup) white sugar and a pinch of kosher salt until dissolved, then add the juice of 1 lime. Let cool to room temperature, pour into a 2 L jug, and top up with about 1 L cold water or ice. Chill for at least 1 hour.

### Cold-brewed jug

Smoother and less bitter, with no kettle, but it takes a day or two. Put 6 bags Ceylon black tea in a 2 L glass or plastic jug with 2 L cold tap water, and steep in the fridge for 24–48 hours. Remove the bags, then stir in 100 g (½ cup) white sugar and 80 ml (⅓ cup) lime juice, lemon juice, or a mix until the sugar dissolves. Serve over ice.

Steep it in the fridge, not on the counter. Tea left at room temperature for hours is the "sun tea" pattern food safety agencies warn against: lukewarm tea is a good home for bacteria.

### Weight-based batch

350 g strong black tea, 40 g 1:1 simple syrup, and 30 g citrus juice (3:1 lime to lemon). Stir over ice in a pitcher rather than shaking. Scales cleanly to any size.
