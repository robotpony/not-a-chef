This is the rich build. Where the drive-in deluxe is all contrast, this one leans into salt and fat: two thin, soft patties with a slice of processed cheese melted between them. Pickles, ketchup, mustard, and onion keep it from going heavy. It's the one case where cheese earns its place on a burger: a thin slice of Kraft Deluxe on a McDouble or a Quarter Pounder.

## Why these picks

- **Two thin patties, not one thick one.** Twice the crust, and the cheese melts between them.
- **Double-ground beef.** It gives the soft, almost pillowy texture of a fast-food patty instead of a coarse, chewy one.
- **Processed cheese.** It melts smooth into a thin patty. Use half a slice of Kraft Deluxe; a full one is too thick.
- **5:1 ketchup to mustard.** The fast-food sweet-savoury balance.
- **Pickle and mustard.** The only sharp and sour on the burger, so they can't be skipped.

## How it fails

- **Heavy:** no pickle or mustard to cut the fat.
- **Coarse, chewy patty:** the beef was single-ground.

## Variations

- **Bacon double cheese:** add crisp bacon. Only if it's crisp; chewy bacon pulls the burger apart. Water-frying gives flatter, more evenly crisp strips (see [[Reverse cooking: reverse sear and water frying]]).
- **Single:** one 45 g patty on a small bun, the drive-thru hamburger (see the [[McDouble]] variations).
