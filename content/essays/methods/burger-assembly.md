The patty brings salt, fat, and savoury depth. Everything else on the burger is there to contrast it: something cold and crunchy (lettuce, raw onion, pickle) and something sharp (onion, mustard, pickle, vinegar). Skip the cold crunch and the burger is one note. Skip the sharpness and it turns heavy by the third bite. Get both, on a bun that fits, and even a mediocre patty works.

## The variables

- **The bun.** Soft and simple, not large or dense, and the same size as the patty (see [[Chef John's hamburger buns]]). Toast it, cut side down in a dry non-stick pan over medium heat, while the burger pan heats. For a fast-food burger, press the bun a little flat.
- **Cold crunch.** Lots of finely shredded iceberg, loose rather than packed. Pickles, sliced thin or lengthwise.
- **Sharp.** Raw onion, sliced paper thin: white, yellow, or purple. Onion is the most important topping on the burger. Two forms beat one: raw for sharpness, plus onion sautéed in the beef fat for sweetness.
- **Sour.** Double-sour pickles. They're hard to buy and easy to make: sprinkle citric acid over ordinary dill pickles and let them sit.
- **Juicy.** Ripe tomato, sliced thin. Skip it when tomatoes aren't good.
- **Cheese and bacon.** Optional. A thin slice of processed cheese melts into a thin patty; bacon only if it's crisp, never chewy.
- **Black pepper.** A crack over the top layer.

## Order

Bottom up, with the sauce against the bread so it soaks into the bun, not the lettuce:

1. Bottom bun, sauce
2. Patty, cheese
3. Pickles, onion
4. Tomato, lettuce
5. Sauce or mayo on the top bun, then the top bun

The exact order changes by build (see [[Drive-in deluxe burger]], [[Fast-food double burger]], [[Grilled onion burger]]).

## Rest it in paper

Wrap the finished burger in burger paper or parchment, or cover it in a pan with a lid, and let it sit for a minute or a few. It steams gently, the bun softens, and the flavours settle. It tastes better than the same burger straight off the griddle. Then eat it right away; burgers hold badly after that.

## How it fails

- **Soggy bottom bun:** the bun wasn't toasted, or the wettest layers sat on it.
- **One note:** no cold crunch.
- **Heavy by the third bite:** nothing sharp. Add onion, mustard, or a sharper pickle.
- **Falls apart, or can't be bitten:** too tall, or the patty and bun don't match. A burger has to be navigable.
