I plan a week of meals by picking proteins and vegetables, stocking the pantry and fridge, and planning base sauces and preparations that can be used in several different ways. I [[Win the fridge|win the fridge]] when I use up all of the perishable ingredients, either in meals or in foods I can freeze for lazier days.

## Bites, dishes, meals

In *Ratatouille*, Remy tastes a strawberry and a piece of cheese together, and his head fills with colour and music. A single bite works like that. A dish is more so: several bites built to go together. A meal is more so again: dishes paired so they play off each other. Planning is dreaming in flavours and pairings, just at a larger scale each step up.

Planning a week runs the same ladder from the top down. I plan the meals first, for pairing and variety. The meals decide the dishes. The dishes decide the bases: the chicken, the beef, the stock, and the vegetables that get cooked on Sunday and pulled apart all week.

## An example week for 2–4 people

1. Pick a few proteins. I plan on about 140 g (5 oz) per person per meal. An example week:

    - 2 whole chickens
    - 1.36 kg (3 lb) of ground beef
    - 1 roast: whatever's cheap, usually under 2 kg (4.4 lb) unless we have guests

2. Stock the pantry and fridge.

    - 2–3 onions, 1–2 heads of garlic, 3–4 shallots, 1 knob of ginger
    - Salad greens and a few raw vegetables
    - 3–4 robust vegetables for roasting or steaming (broccoli, cauliflower, etc.)
    - 1–2 starches (rice, potatoes, noodles, bread)
    - Spices and special sauces
        - Salad dressings
        - Rice bowl sauces
        - Dipping sauces
        - Plate sauces
    - Oils

3. Pick one or two dinners for the week that share ingredients. Every other meal builds on the prep for those, so the week uses up what they leave behind without repeating the same dinner.

Sunday prep would typically look like:

- Break down and roast the chicken

    - Cut each bird into breasts, thighs and drumsticks, and wings
    - Set the wings aside for a wing night; the tips go into [[Chicken or turkey stock]] with the necks and backbones
    - Dry brine 3–4 hours and season however you like (Indian spices, herbes de Provence, etc.)
    - Roast on sheet pans to 74°C (165°F)
    - For salads, casseroles, and roast veg plates

- Braise the ground beef (cook it in a little liquid until tender, then brown it; see [[Beef base]])

    - Season lightly
    - Let cool and package up in meal-sized portions
    - For [[Meat sauce (AKA American bolognese)|meat sauce]], taco beef, [[Canadian chili|chili]], dressed beans

- Cook the roast (oven or braise)

    - Dry brine for 24 hours (often cooking 1–2 days later), starting this Friday or Saturday
    - Slice and package up for lunches (sandwiches, soups)
    - Freeze the extra for sandwiches and snacks

- Roast the vegetables, 2–3 days' worth at a time
    - Sheet pan, veg, oil, salt, and seasoning

## The week

Sunday's prep turns into five dinners that don't feel like the same meal five times. The roast covers lunches all week.

| Day | Dinner | From Sunday's prep |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | Roast chicken plates with roast veg and rice | Chicken thighs and drumsticks, roast veg |
| Tuesday | [[Meat sauce (AKA American bolognese)\|Meat sauce]] over pasta, with a green salad | Half the beef base, salad greens |
| Wednesday | Chicken salad, or a casserole if it's cold out | Chicken breasts, the rest of the roast veg |
| Thursday | Tacos or [[Canadian chili\|chili]] | The rest of the beef base |
| Friday | Wing night | The wings, while the tips and backbones simmer into stock |

Each dinner leans on one or two bases and adds something fresh: a salad, a starch, a sauce. The bases do the slow work once; the weeknight cooking is assembly.

## The takeaway

Plan from the top down, then cook from the bottom up. Pick the meals first, for pairing and variety, and let them decide the dishes and the Sunday prep. Then cook the bases once and spend the week assembling. If Thursday's fridge still holds half a bag of spinach and a container of beef, that isn't a failed plan; it's Friday's dinner, or something for the freezer. The week is won when the perishables are gone and nobody ate the same dinner twice.
