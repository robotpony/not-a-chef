- Doneness is a function of time and temperature. The chart above aims for optimum texture and safety, and requires holding (i.e., reaching + maintaining) the stated temperature for a minimum of 3 minutes. These temperatures are lower than USDA/CFIA recommendations, which assume no holding time.
- Some cuts of meat are more suited for higher internal temperatures and longer cook times to soften and dissolve connective tissues (e.g., chuck roast, pork shoulder/butt). Some roasts are more suitable at more rare internal temperatures (e.g., bottom roasts, loins, most steaks, etc.).
- Most meats should rest for ~5% of their cooking time, to maximise moistness (e.g., steaks for 5 minutes, roasts for 10-20 minutes)
- Most cuts of fish do not have connective tissue and are optimal at rare-medium. Fish and seafood does not require any significant rest time.
- Poultry may have streaks of red in dark meat near the bones, even when fully cooked, often caused by weak, broken, or cut bones and not due to insufficient doneness. This is common in bone-in poultry that has been frozen, and is caused by pigment leached out of the marrow of the bones. This is safe to consume and has no strong flavour, even if it appears unpleasant.
