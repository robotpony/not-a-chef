## Thursday, November 19th

Made that batch of [[Roasted cauliflower, turmeric, and ginger soup]]. I waited a few days longer than planned, but the cauliflower was still in good shape. Some minor adjustments to the recipe:

- I put the seasoning in with the aromatics (after the onion, before the garlic)
- I roasted the cauli by quartering, as I was being a bit lazy
- Also roasted up the onion root end (dirty parts removed), and the garlic (root removed), both with olive oil + salt
- Added fennel: it needed something towards warming, but sharper

The soup went together quickly. It tasted good, but it needed all of the coconut milk (as it was a bit salty). That's fine, but next time I would soak the pork longer and pull it out before starting the onions. I also forgot to hold back the pork before zipping it up, but it seemed to emulsify things pretty well.

Next time I may deglaze with wine. I didn't have any on hand that I wanted to open, and my other choices felt like the wrong flavour (sherry, sake, flavoured liqueurs).

## Saturday, November 7th

We've been sick on and off for a few weeks, so we wanted some comfort food. Tonight we're making boerenkool, mostly from the pantry and freezer. It's 1.5 kg of potatoes, with 1–2 onions diced, 1 large tin of kale, and a smoked sausage if you eat 'em. It's old-school comfort food, relatively fast, and doesn't require fresh ingredients. And it has nutrition: it's loaded with vitamins C and K, and potassium. A lack of micronutrients is a problem with most fast and prepared foods, as they've evolved, so these old-school fast foods are both comforting and not horrible for you. This is why one of the goals of this cookbook is to show how food can be fast, nutritious, and even comforting.
