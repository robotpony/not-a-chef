Tomato Soup

Serves4
Prep10 min
Cook25 min
Total35 min

Warm the olive oil in a heavy pot over medium heat. Add onion and cook until softened and translucent, about 8 minutes.

Add garlic and cook until fragrant, about 1 minute.

Add tomatoes (crush them by hand as they go in) and broth. Bring to a simmer and cook, uncovered, for 15 minutes.

Blend until smooth with an immersion blender or in batches in a stand blender.

Return to the pot over low heat. Stir in the cream and season with salt and pepper. Taste and adjust.

To serve

With crusty bread or a grilled cheese. A drizzle of olive oil and cracked pepper on top.

Notes
  • Tinned fire-roasted tomatoes add smokiness.
  • For a vegan version, omit cream or use coconut cream.

Bruce's Burger Sauce

Servesmakes ~4 servings
Prep5 min
Total5 min
Mechanic

A 1:1:1 ratio by volume: mayo, diced burger pickles, mustard. Kewpie in place of or alongside regular mayo adds sweetness and acidity.

Stir together. Taste and adjust ratios.

To serve

On Swiss, mozza-style, or deluxe burgers. Good as a spread on both bun halves.

Notes
  • Developed for A&W-style mozza burger. draft, still refining
  • The ratios are starting points; lean more mayo for a milder sauce, more pickle for brightness.
  • A small squeeze of ketchup or splash of pickle brine can soften the mustard edge.

Meatballs

Servesmakes ~1.7 kg (30–35 meatballs)
Prep30 min
Cook20 min
Total50 min
Mechanic

A meatball is a protein base, binders, moisteners (panade), and flavourings. The panade, breadcrumbs soaked in liquid, keeps the meatball from drying out. Grated onion (not diced) disappears into the mix and adds moisture. The percentages below are by weight, relative to 1 kg of ground meat, so the recipe scales to any quantity.

Based on 1 kg ground meat

Combine breadcrumbs and liquid (water, stock, or a mix with cream, buttermilk, or sour cream). Let soak until absorbed.

Grind or measure spices and herbs. Add to the panade along with grated onion and garlic. Mix to combine.

Add ground meat and mix thoroughly, by hand or with a paddle attachment, until the mixture is homogenous and slightly sticky.

Portion into balls (about 50g each for a standard meatball). Place on a rack over a baking sheet.

Bake at 190–220°C (375–425°F) until browned and just cooked through, 15–20 minutes.

Variations

Asian style

Replace some of the liquid with hoisin (3%), soy sauce (6.5%), and sesame oil (1%). Add ginger (1%). Reduce salt (soy takes its place). Skip the Parmesan. Scale back liquid slightly. The mixture should be a little firmer than the Italian version.

Glaze: 60 ml hoisin, 30 ml ketchup, 15 ml honey or maple syrup, 30 ml rice vinegar, 15 ml soy sauce, 5 ml sesame oil. Brush over meatballs in the last 5 minutes of baking. Finish with sesame seeds and sliced scallions.

Notes
  • Freezes well after baking. Cool completely, freeze on a tray, then bag.
  • Can be added to any braise or sauce directly from frozen. Add 10 minutes to simmer time.

Dal Tadka

Serves4
Prep10 min
Cook25 min
Total35 min
Mechanic

Red lentils cook fast and dissolve into a creamy base. The ratio matters: 1 cup lentils to 4–5 cups water. Less water (1:4) gives a thicker dal you can heap on rice; more (1:5) gives something looser.

The tadka is not garnish. Hot fat with spices poured over at the end is the flavour. Don't skip it.

Dal

  • add split peas for more texture (+10 min pressure cooking time)

Add everything to a pot. Bring to a rolling boil, then lower heat, cover, and simmer gently for 20 minutes. Stir and lightly mash. The lentils should dissolve into a smooth, creamy texture. Taste and adjust salt.

Tadka

Melt the fat in a small pan over medium-high heat. Add the cumin seeds. When they sizzle, add the garlic, onion, and hing. Fry until golden and fragrant, about 3–4 minutes. Pour over the dal and cover for a minute to let it infuse.

To serve

Warm basmati rice, plain raita, a crunchy salad (tomatoes, onion, cucumber, lime, salt), lime pickle on the side.

Variations

Build a base first

Cook diced onion in oil until golden, then add garlic, ginger, coriander, and turmeric before the lentils go in. The lentils absorb a more developed flavour. Finish with lemon juice and garam masala off heat. The tadka is simpler: ghee, cumin, hing, a dried red chile. Use 1:4 ratio for a thicker result. Vice

Coconut cream dal

Use yellow split peas instead of masoor (400g, no soak needed). Sauté a diced onion in oil until soft, add 2 tbsp ginger and 1 tsp crushed red chilli, then stir in the spices (2 tsp garam masala, 2 tsp turmeric, ½ tsp cinnamon, 1 tsp garlic powder, salt, pepper) for one minute. Add 1 tsp honey, a 400g can of crushed tomatoes, a 400ml can of coconut cream, and 3 cups water. Stir in the split peas and simmer covered for 80 minutes, stirring occasionally. No tadka. Richer and sweeter than the base recipe; pairs better with roti and mango chutney than rice. VJ Cooks

Go deeper

Use toor dal (yellow split pigeon peas) instead of masoor; nuttier, slower to cook (40 min simmered, or 8 min pressure cooker). Build the base separately with more spice: coriander seeds, cloves, Kashmiri chilli powder.

For smokiness, try the Dhungar technique: nestle a small metal bowl in the pot, drop in a lit piece of charcoal, add a splash of ghee, lid on for 5 minutes. Andy Cooks

Notes
  • Masoor (red lentils) need no soaking. Toor dal benefits from 30 minutes in water first.
  • Hing smells aggressive in the jar; it mellows completely in hot fat and adds a savoury depth.
  • After pouring the tadka, spoon a bit of dal into the pan, stir to pick up the remaining spiced fat, and return it to the pot.
  • Leftovers thicken considerably. Loosen with water when reheating.

Spice levels across the three recipes

This recipe sits in the middle of the range. Vice adds ground coriander and a dried chile. Andy Cooks goes furthest: green chillies in the base, Kashmiri chilli powder in the tadka. VJ Cooks is the warmest and sweetest: cinnamon, honey, and coconut cream push it into a different register entirely. The garam masala and cinnamon in this recipe are borrowed from VJ; omit them for the simpler original version. The 1 tablespoon ginger is the floor across all four recipes for the flavour to register.

Pulled Pork

ServesTBD
Prep15 min (plus 12–24 hr rub rest)
Cook1 hr
TotalTBD
Mechanic

The pork braises in stock and aromatics only; the barbecue sauce is a separate reduction of vinegar, ketchup, and liquid smoke, not the strained braising liquid. Keeping them apart means the sauce flavour doesn't depend on how much fat and pork juice ends up in the pot.

  1. Rub: coat pork shoulders evenly with the adapted spice rub. Refrigerate 12–24 hours.
  2. Braise: combine chicken stock, onion, and garlic in the pressure cooker pot. Nestle in the rubbed pork.
  3. Cook: bring to high pressure, then cook 45–60 minutes. Release pressure naturally for 20 minutes. Avoid quick releasing here so the liquid doesn't get too cloudy.
  4. Sauce: as the pork depressurizes, simmer cider vinegar, ketchup, and liquid smoke in a saucepan until slightly thickened, 10 to 15 minutes. Season with salt, pepper, and brown sugar to taste.
  5. Finish: shred the pork, discarding fat. Skim fat from the braising liquid; stir a little into the sauce for extra flavour and body, or discard.
To serve

Stir some sauce into the shredded pork, serve on buns with the rest on the side.

Notes
  • A 6L pressure cooker holds 2 boneless shoulders (or 1 bone-in).
  • Untested as split-liquid version. Sauce yield and reduction time haven't been measured; update once tested. draft
