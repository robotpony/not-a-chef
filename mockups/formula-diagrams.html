Formula diagrams

A one-row picture of a recipe's ratio: icons for the parts, joined by +, : or →, with the quantity under each icon. It sits next to the sentence it illustrates and doesn't replace it. The sentence stays for search, screen readers, Obsidian and print.

The approach follows Scorekeep's rules pages: Markdown names the diagram, and the renderer draws it from one shared kit of marks, all shown on one page (§6). Ink lines, one soft fill, and quantities in the mono face. No new colours.

§1

Veg patties, in the body column

The worked example from the brief. The fenced block goes right after the Mechanic's opening sentence, and the swap list keeps doing its job below it.

Mechanic

A veg patty is a formula, not a fixed recipe: per can of beans, 1 cup cooked starch, 1 cup vegetables, ½ cup breadcrumbs, and 1 egg. The beans are the body, the starch makes the mix tacky, and the binder holds it together in the pan.

Water is what breaks a veg patty, so dry or cook every wet ingredient first: pat the beans dry, sauté the vegetables, and smash anything round so it can't roll loose.

?

The = result slot is optional. It turns "a list of parts" into "these make that," which is the bit the sentence leaves to the reader. It costs one slot of width. Without it, the row fits the sidebar at four slots (§4).

§2

Three kinds, one component

Additive ships first. Ratio and sequence use the same slot, with a different operator and a different emphasis.

Additive: +

Parts that go together in one bowl. The quantity is a measure, so it sits small, under the label.

Ratio: :

Here the numbers are the whole point, so they lead in large mono and the label goes underneath. An optional parts bar draws the proportion, which you can't see in "3 : 2 : 1" at a glance.

Sequence: →

For the shape of a method, not every step in it. Each slot gets one verb and an optional italic note. If it needs more than four or five slots, use numbered steps instead.

§3

What the author writes

A fenced formula block: one slot per line, and the operator at the start of each line after the first. In Obsidian it still reads as the formula, just in plain text.

```formula
can          | Beans   | 1 can         | black, pinto, chickpea
+ bowl       | Starch  | 1 cup         | rice, mash, quinoa
+ carrot     | Veg     | 1 cup         | carrot, peas, corn
+ crumbs egg | Binder  | ½ cup + 1 egg | or gluten + oats
= patty      | Patties | 6
```
```formula
flour   | Flour     | 3
: butter | Fat       | 2
: water  | Water     | 1
caption: Pie crust, by weight
bar: yes
```
FieldMeaning
iconOne or two kit keys (§6), separated by a space. An unknown key draws an empty circle and prints a build warning, so a typo shows up in review instead of breaking the page.
labelOne short word, sentence case ("Veg", not "Vegetables"). The same label is used at every size, so it has to fit the sidebar.
quantitySame units as the recipe text (Canadian, metric first). In a : block it's the ratio number. In a → block, anything not starting with a digit renders as the italic note.
swapsOptional fourth field, shown in small italics under the quantity so the reader knows the slot takes alternatives. Commas split it into one swap per line, at every size, so nothing breaks mid-list. Up to three. Free text otherwise, so "or gluten + oats" prints as written. The icon draws the worked example's choice.
+ : → =One operator per block (+, : or →), plus an optional closing = result. Mixing operators in one row is refused, because it stops being glanceable.
caption / barOptional trailing keys. bar only applies to ratios.
?

Who writes it. Claude adds the block when you ask ("diagram the mechanic"), or when it finds a %% diagram %% comment in the file. Obsidian hides that comment, and Hugo strips it. The recipe-writing skill would get one rule: only diagram a ratio that's already stated in the text.

§4

Sidebar and phone

On recipe pages, ## Mechanic is moved into the right sidebar, about 18.6rem wide. A formula written there has to fit that column, so it gets a compact density: 34px icons and tighter operators. Four slots fit, and a fifth wraps.

Mechanic

A veg patty is a formula: per can of beans, 1 cup cooked starch, 1 cup vegetables, ½ cup breadcrumbs, and 1 egg.

recipe sidebar · 18.6rem · compact
Mechanic
phone · 360px · five slots wrap, operator stays with its slot
?

Labels stay short everywhere (decided 2026-10-06), so the sidebar never needs a separate short label. Quantities can still run long: "½ cup + 1 egg" wraps to two lines in the sidebar. On a wrap, each operator moves to the next line with its slot, so no row ever ends on a dangling +.

§5

On recipe cards

A 24px strip in the card shows a recipe's shape while you browse: icons and operators only, with no labels or quantities, except the numbers in a ratio, which are the whole point. It takes the place of the intro's third line, so a card with a formula is the same height as one without. The card's stat line stays pinned to the bottom either way.

?

What it costs. One line of intro on the cards that have a formula. Cards don't render the recipe body, so the card partial needs its own way to get the strip. A small partial that re-parses the block from .RawContent would work.

Unevenness. In a grid, rows already stretch to the tallest card, so a strip on some cards and not others reads as "these have a formula," not as misalignment. Swaps and the = result never appear on the card.

§6

Icon kit

A 48-unit grid, 2-unit round-capped strokes in currentColor, one --accent-soft fill on each object's body, and --surface on lids and tops. That's the same rule as the favicon: existing tokens only, readable at 34px, and it follows dark mode for free. This first pass has 19 keys. Scorekeep's equivalent is the in-app Design guide, and this section is meant to become that page.

Sizes

Full (48), compact (34), and a 24px inline size that isn't used yet, as a check that the marks survive small.

§7

How it would be built

Everything happens in the Hugo build, and the page needs no JavaScript.
