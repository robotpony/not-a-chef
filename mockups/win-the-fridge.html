Special treatment for #win-the-fridge

Three pieces: a general glossary/definition mechanism (inline popovers, reused for any term — brand names like Kewpie mayonnaise, technique terms, etc.), a new reference essay format that's where full definitions live (win-the-fridge gets the first one), and a one-off green "principle chip" that marks win-the-fridge wherever it appears as a tag, since it names a governing idea rather than just describing a dish.

Everything below reuses real component CSS from assets/css/custom.css — colors and layout should match the live site exactly except where marked NEW.

A second inline-reference style, alongside .wikilink: dotted underline (vs. wikilink's solid one), no trailing arrow, hover/focus reveals a short definition plus a link through. Win the fridge principle

14 recipes
Definition

Win the fridge is the practice of using every scrap of food before it goes to waste — working through the fridge, freezer, and pantry regularly instead of letting things quietly expire. It's a frugal habit and an ethical one: food waste is avoidable, and being well-fed is a privilege worth not squandering.

It extends nose-to-tail and root-to-stem thinking to everyday cooking — every cut from a butchered animal, every part of a vegetable (stalks, leaves, peels), and anything else that would otherwise be thrown out but is still delicious and nourishing.
