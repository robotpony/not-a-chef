Drink diagrams

Three ways to draw a drink, shown with the Knotty Irishman. The working pick is B, the glass, with C as its card thumbnail. A is free today but says less.

A · Formula ratio (exists today)

The formula fence with the : operator and bar: yes. It needs only new icons (bottle, mug, glass, ice). It shows the spirit ratio but not the build: no glass, no float, no ice.

B · The glass (new glass fence)

A section through the glass with bands drawn to scale in pour order, labels on the right, a bracket for what gets stirred, and garnish on top. This is the classic bar spec card in the layer kit's line and fill style. Like stack, rows run top first.

B1 · Hot, pour bands

B2 · Over ice, one blended band

Source (B1)

```glass
nutmeg   | Nutmeg          | grated
cream    | Cream, whipped  | 60 ml | float
coffee   | Hot coffee      | 120 ml
liqueur  | Frangelico      | 15 ml
whiskey  | Irish whiskey   | 30 ml
liqueur  | Baileys         | 60 ml
glass: mug 300 ml
stir: 3-6
caption: 300 ml mug, warmed
```

How it reads

Rows run fill | label | amount | note, top first like stack. Band heights come from the amounts, scaled to glass: (mug, rocks, highball, wine, coupe, pitcher). stir: brackets the rows that mix; B2's single band is the same fence with all four in one row. A garnish row (nutmeg, lemon wheel, mint) draws above the rim instead of as a band, and ice: yes adds the cubes.

Pick between B1 pour bands (shows the build order, but stirred drinks don't really stay layered) and B2 one band per mix (honest about the glass, loses the proportions). A middle option: pour bands with a stir bracket, as in B1.
