Where the burgers lineage goes

Since the blueprint page moved onto the reading layout, the lineage track lives in a 700px body column. It still fits, but its labels already stack two rows deep, and the 1980s and 2000s pairs crowd each other. This mock tries it in the sidebar, running down the page two ways and across it two ways. It also shows a full-width band above both columns and today's version, for comparison.

Below the definition either way, as decided. The builds and dates are the real ones (era 1922 to 2010, so the track runs 1910 to 2020). Parts and build cards are trimmed to placeholders.

1 · The page

Vertical: to scale: a spine down the sidebar, oldest at the top, each build placed by its year (3px a year), decades on the left, name and year on the right; where dots crowd, names are nudged down with a short leader. Vertical: list: a row per build down a spine, evenly spaced, with the gap above a row growing with the years since the last (capped), and the long gaps labelled. Strip and rows: a dot strip as wide as the sidebar, with a row per build under it (year, then name). Hover a row and its dot lights up; hover a dot and its row does. Angled labels: the track itself, with each name slanted up from its dot and nudged apart where dots crowd. Band: today's track, but across the full 1100px above both columns. Today: as built. On Phone the sidebar drops below the page, so the sidebar versions fall back to the body's vertical list.

      2 · Choices

      Recommendation

      Vertical: to scale. The sidebar is narrow but tall, so the timeline runs down it, oldest first, the way the page reads. It keeps what the track is for, the shape of the history (two early builds, one mid-century, a cluster from the eighties on), and every name sits level and unslanted beside its dot. It's about 350px tall for a century of builds, about what the parts list takes in the body. Hover a name and its dot lights up.

      Vertical: list is the compact fallback: shorter, simpler markup, and close to the phone list as built, but the spacing only hints at the gaps. It's the better fit if a blueprint's builds span a few centuries, where to scale would run long.

      • Strip and rows is the horizontal pick: the strip shows the shape and the rows carry the names, but the eye has to go between the two.
      • Angled labels is the literal version: a real track with names on it. Even at 60° it needs about 56px of right margin so "Smash burger" doesn't run out of the sidebar, which shortens the track, and the 2008 and 2010 dots are 6px apart, so their labels have to be pushed apart with leader lines. It reads, but it's busy for a sidebar, and slanted text is slower to read.
      • Band gives today's track the full 1100px, which is the most room it can get. But it pushes the parts and the sidebar down, and it's the only thing on the page that breaks the two-column frame.
      • Phones and tablets: under 1024px the sidebar drops below the page, so a sidebar lineage would end up at the bottom, after the builds. I'd keep the body's vertical list there (as on phones today) and show the sidebar version only on desktop, the same way "On this page" is desktop only.
      • Order in the sidebar: after the counts, before "On this page". "Lineage" leaves "On this page" at desktop widths, since it's already in view.
      • Template side is Fugu (fugu-agent): blueprint/lineage.html gets a sidebar form; blueprint/page.html calls it from the sidebar and keeps the vertical list in the body below 1024px.
