An icon for methods

Methods get a concept page like Win the fridge and Test kitchen (docs/METHODS.md), so they need a mark that sits next to those two on a card's top row without looking like either.

Every candidate here is drawn the way pot-steam.svg is: a 48-unit grid, round 2.4 strokes in currentColor. Each one is shown at chip size (12px), where it has to work.

1 · What it has to sit next to

Win the fridgeprinciple · green · cycle arrows
Test kitchenstatus · blue · pot and steam
Plant-based (later)principle? · green · leaf

2 · Candidates

A method is the pattern behind many dishes, so the best marks say "one way, many results" or "built in layers" rather than "cooking" in general. A whisk or a knife would fit any page on the site.

3 · Colour

The palette has five hues and four are spoken for: orange→red (accent), yellow (cuisine), blue (test kitchen), green (principle). STYLE.md rules out a sixth. Two ways to colour the chip without one:

A · Accent (recommended)

STYLE.md says the accent means "interactive, or teaches you something." A method is the part of the site that teaches. Fill --accent-soft, text --accent-strong.

B · Neutral outline

No hue at all: ink text, a faint outline, the icon does the work. Quieter, but it sits close to the plain tag chips.

4 · On cards

The card's top row has two slots before +N. A method page is an essay, so its card has no cuisine chip; the method chip takes the first slot. Shown with each candidate, accent colour.

5 · All the marks together

The full set once plant-based arrives, as they'd appear in a recipe page's tag row. Recommended candidate shown.

6 · Recommendation

Fan-out, in the accent colour. One source branching to three results is the definition of a method in one picture, it reads at 12px, and it's unlike the cycle arrows and the pot. Layers is the runner-up; it ties to the stack diagrams, but it's close to a generic "layers" UI icon and overlaps with burgers specifically.

Plant-based, for the later task: green is taken by principle, and there's no sixth hue. Plant-based is a value like Win the fridge, so it can share the principle green and be told apart by the leaf. That makes green mean "a value this recipe holds," not "Win the fridge" alone, which STYLE.md would need to say.
