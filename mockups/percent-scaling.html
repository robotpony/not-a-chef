Percent scaling

On a percentage recipe, you start from what you have: "I have 1.36 kg of pork, what do I add?" Today's gear only offers multiples from 1× to 5×, so the cook does that division themselves.

This mock tries the amount field in the gear callout (A) and above the table (B). I recommend B: the field is on the page where you'll see it, and on a phone nothing covers the amounts while you type. Every frame is live and shares one amount, so type in any of them. The recipe switch at the top swaps in the other two recipes with this kind of table.

§1

What's there today

The site already recognizes these tables and scales their amount column, so this is a new way in, not a new engine. Three things don't fit a percentage recipe:

TodayWhat goes wrong
Slider, 1× to 5× in half steps1.36 kg of pork is 1.36×, which the slider can't hit. It can't go below 1× either, so half a batch of pizza dough is out of reach.
1–2 kg ground pork scales as a rangeAt 2× the line reads 2–4 kg. The base stops being a number you chose and turns into a wider range.
Imperial converts every amountPork in pounds is useful, but 3 g of sage shows as ⅛ oz and 1.3 g of white pepper as 0.05 oz.
§2

A: in the gear callout

The amount field replaces the slider, with the ratified callout around it. Every recipe keeps one control in one place, and the page stays exactly as quiet as it is now. The cost is that you have to know to open it. On desktop the callout only covers the Notes column, so the amounts stay readable; on a phone it covers the amounts in the first few rows. Close it and a 1.36× stays beside the gear, so a scaled table never looks like the written one.

§3

B: above the table Recommended

The field sits directly above the table it drives, so it's the first thing you read in the section and you can see every row change as you type. The gear stays, with Units only. Percentage recipes get a visible control that other recipes don't, which is fair: the amount you start with is the first decision on these recipes. It's hidden in print, where the table prints at the amount shown.

§3b

B, round 2: lined up with the columns Chosen 2026-11-04

Bruce's notes on the built bar: the amount over the weight column, the name over the name column, and revert as just an icon beside the units, so the controls read along with the table. To line up at any width, the bar becomes a row of the table itself, above the headings. The multiplier sits over the % column. The box goes, since the row now belongs to the table, and the digits line up with the amounts below. The gear goes on these pages, since Units has almost nothing left to do there.

Teriyaki has no Notes column, so on desktop its units sit beside the amount, in the same cell. On a phone the units would run off the right edge, so on every recipe they stack under the amount. Try the recipe switch at the top.

§4

Also worth a look: B, in the 100% row

You didn't ask for this one. The base's own amount cell becomes the field, so no extra row of controls appears and the number you edit is where the number already was. It's tidier, but harder to spot: a field among 14 rows doesn't announce that it drives the others. It also crowds the cell on a phone. I'd go with the bar unless the bar feels heavy to you.

§6

Which recipes get it, and how the numbers work

The rule has no new syntax: an ingredient table with a % column and a 100% row. That row is the base, and its name and unit fill in the field. Lazy pizza dough and Teriyaki sauce already qualify. Breakfast sausage keeps its pork outside the table, so it needs one edit, with the pork moving into the table:

- 1–2 kg ground pork shoulder (80/20 or 70/30); pork belly also works

#### Meat and spice mix (% of meat weight)

| Ingredient | % | Per 1 kg | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ground pork shoulder | 100% | 1 kg | 80/20 or 70/30; pork belly also works |
| Water, cold | 7.5% | 75 g | |
| Milk powder | 2% | 20 g | Don't skip it |
Decision in the mockWhy
Amounts scale from the written amount, not the %The two disagree slightly: yeast is 1.7% of 600 g, or 10.2 g, and the recipe says 10 g. Scaling the written amount keeps the tested numbers exact at the written batch, and the % column stays as the explanation.
Under 20 reads to 0.1; 20 and up to the whole number; 1000 and up as kg or LToday's 2 decimals would show 1.77 g of white pepper. Written amounts like 12.5 ml and 7.5 g still read as written.
The base has its own g, kg, lb segments (ml, L, cup for shoyu)Pork comes labelled in pounds, and the spices still go on a gram scale. The Units setting only picks the field's starting unit here; the amounts stay as written.
Any amount, no 1× floor or 5× capHalf a batch of dough works. Empty or zero holds the last good amounts instead of blanking the table.
Amount is remembered per recipe, with a "Back to" linkSame idea as today's per-recipe scale. With JS off the table reads as written, as it does now.

§8

Decisions

1

Placement? Decided 2026-11-04: B. Then round 2 the same day (§3b): the bar becomes a table row, with no gear on these pages.

2

Breakfast sausage's pork? Decided 2026-11-04: it moves into the table as a 100% row at 1 kg, and "1–2 kg" goes, since the field now does that job.

3

Imperial on these tables? Decided 2026-11-04: table amounts stay as written, and the base can be entered in lb or cups. A saved Units setting still applies to the Method's temperatures and quantities, with no control on these pages (round 2).

4

Quantities that shouldn't scale? Decided 2026-11-04: reword the lines, with no new syntax. So far that's "Fry 1–2 tbsp of the mixture" in Breakfast sausage and "three 250 g pizzas" in Lazy pizza dough. New recipes can hit this again.
