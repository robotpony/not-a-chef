Methods list and blueprint page: who shows what

Proposal, 2026-10-12 · mockups/methods-list-and-blueprint.html · against STYLE.md "Methods and blueprint pages"

The methods list (/methods/) and a blueprint's page (/methods/burgers/) show the same build cards at the same size. This mock splits the job: the list is a map (what each blueprint is, its parts, and the names of its builds), and the blueprint page is the shelf (the builds as cards, with their drawings). The blueprint page takes the list's compact parts block in place of its long one, so the two pages share one component. One-pan pasta is added as a second blueprint to see how both pages hold up with more than burgers on them.

Each page then has one thing the other doesn't. On the list it's the other blueprints and the standalone techniques; on the blueprint page it's the lineage and the build cards. The parts block is the same on both pages, which is fine: it's the blueprint's table of contents, and you want it in both places.

02The methods listProposed

Each blueprint is one block: name, description, counts, the parts, and a line of build names at the bottom. It's one block per blueprint, so a blueprint with twelve builds doesn't push the next one down a screen. Draft builds have a dashed outline. Switch the builds line to compare.

04Combining the two pagesNot proposed

The other way out is one page: the methods list grows a section per blueprint with everything in it, and the blueprint pages go. With one blueprint that's tidy. With two it's already long, since the pasta builds and the burger builds stack one after the other, and each blueprint's lineage has nowhere to go but the body. It also drops the blueprint's own URL, which the build and part pages link back to ("All burgers"), and which the homepage's Methods band links to. Keeping both pages and giving each a different job is cheaper and scales better.

05One-pan pasta, as a blueprint

Drafted from your notes to test the model, not yet in content/. It fits the blueprint shape: the parts are stages in time rather than layers, the way Cooking patties already is for burgers, and each build is the same method with a different sauce. What it shows about the pages:

  • No drawing. A pasta build has no layers to stack, so its card is shorter, with its picks as text instead. On the blueprint page every card is the same kind, so that's fine. On the list, "Small drawings" has nothing to show for pasta, which is why names are the better choice there.
  • No lineage. One-pan pasta is a technique, not a historical style, so its builds have no era and the sidebar has no timeline. The page works without one.
  • Few builds. Two builds against burgers' seven. Showing names on the list keeps the two blocks about the same height either way.

One-pan pasta: pasta cooked in the pan it's sauced in, in just enough water to cover, so the water turns starchy and finishes the sauce the way a restaurant's pasta water does.

One-pan pasta is how a restaurant finishes a pasta dish, done in a single pan at home. On the line, pasta comes out of a pot that's been boiling pasta all day and goes into a pan of sauce with a splash of that water. The water is cloudy with starch, and it tightens the sauce until it clings to the pasta. At home the pot water is clean, so the pasta cooks in the sauce pan itself, in just enough water to cover it, and that little bit of water gets as starchy as the restaurant's. It saves a pot, and the sauce is better for it.

Parts

  • Cooking pasta in the pan. Dry pasta in a wide pan, in salted water just to cover, at a boil. The small amount of water is the point. Options: short (penne, rigatoni), long (spaghetti, linguine), fresh (goes in last).
  • Pasta sauces. Built in the pan, or brought in from a base you made ahead. Options: from a base (meat sauce, marinara), built fresh (cherry tomatoes, garlic, oil), cream.
  • Finishing pasta in the sauce. The sauce goes in when the pasta is 2–3 minutes from done; the pasta finishes in the sauce, and the starch pulls them together. Loosen it with water. Options: tight, loose.
  • Off the heat. Take the pan off the heat, then add whatever heat would spoil: cheese, butter or good oil, fresh herbs, acid. Plate it straight away. Options: cheese and butter, oil and herbs, lemon and chili.

Builds

  • American bolognese pasta. Short pasta finished in meat sauce made from a frozen beef base, with Parmesan off the heat. Recipe: [[Meat sauce (AKA American bolognese)]].
  • Cherry tomato and garlic pasta. Long pasta finished with cherry tomatoes burst in olive oil and garlic, with basil and Parmesan off the heat.
