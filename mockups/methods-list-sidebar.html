Methods list: give it the recipe page's sidebar

The components on /methods/ are settled (the blueprint block, the build tiles, the technique cards). What's off is the page around them: it's the only list page that runs edge to edge with a mix of widths. The callout stops at 72ch and leaves a gap on the right, the blueprint row runs to the far edge, and the techniques fill half a row.

This mock moves the page onto the recipe and reading layout: a body column plus the 20.6rem sidebar. Everything stays inside the body column, and the builds get a way to hold more than three without spilling out of it.

1 · Why the win-the-fridge page doesn't feel off

Its callout has the same gap on the right, but everything under it is one thing at one width: a search box and a full four-across grid. The full grid gives the eye a right edge, so the short callout reads as a heading block above it. The methods page has three widths stacked on top of each other (callout, full-bleed row, half row of cards), so no edge holds.

A sidebar fixes this the way it does on recipes: it supplies the right edge, and the body column is narrow enough that the callout, the blueprint, and two cards across all fill it. It also gives the page's counts and "On this page" a home, which a map-like page wants anyway.

2 · With a sidebar

Try the switches. Definition: lede is plain body text like a recipe's intro; callout keeps today's box, now filling the column; sidebar moves it beside the page like a recipe's Mechanic, leaving a one-line lede. Builds: rail shows three tiles across the column and scrolls when there are more (the fourth peeks, arrows appear); grid wraps into new rows of three; list is a row per build with a small stack. Count fills in three made-up builds to see how each holds up (they reuse the real drawings).

3 · Without a sidebar, for comparison

The other way to balance it: keep the full width, but give the callout a partner on its right (the counts, where the header count was), and let the builds rail scroll inside the row instead of growing. It works, but each new page type would need its own right-hand filler, and the techniques still sit in half a row until there are four.

4 · Choices

Recommendation

The sidebar layout (§2), definition as a lede, builds as a rail. The sidebar gives the page the same frame as recipes and essays, so methods stop being the odd one out. With a sidebar beside it the boxed callout is one box too many (the blueprint is a box, the tiles are boxes); as plain text it reads as the page's intro, the way a recipe's does, and the kind is already in the nav and the title.

The rail keeps a blueprint's row at one height no matter how many builds it has, and the tiles stay at one scale for comparing. Past three it hides the rest behind a scroll, but that's fine here: this page is the map, and "Compare picks →" goes to the blueprint page, where all the builds line up part by part. If a blueprint ever has eight or more builds, the list is the better fit, and the two could switch over at a count.

  • Sidebar contents: search (as everywhere), a counts rail in the date-rows style (methods, blueprints, builds, parts written), "On this page" with each blueprint under Blueprints, and the further-reading list, which moves out of the body. Nothing is pinned to the sidebar that a phone reader needs first: under 1024px it drops below the page as on recipes.
  • Parts in two columns: at body width a single column of six parts leaves most of the block empty; two columns, numbered down then across, halve its height. One column again on phones.
  • Builds under the block, not beside it: beside it, three tiles don't fit the body column. The tiles themselves are unchanged.
  • Rail behaviour: three fill the column exactly; with four or more, the tiles shrink a little so the next one peeks under a fade, and arrow buttons appear beside "Compare picks →". Swipe on touch, scroll-snapped, no visible scrollbar.
  • The methods tag page shares this partial (method/list.html), so it gets the same layout. Template side is Fugu (fugu-agent); the sidebar can reuse reading-sidebar.html's pieces.
